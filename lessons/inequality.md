# Inequalities

## Learning Goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

* Understand what an inequality is.
* Interpret inequality symbols.
* Solve one-step inequalities.
* Solve multi-step inequalities.
* Solve inequalities with variables on both sides.
* Solve inequalities with parentheses.
* Solve inequalities involving fractions and decimals.
* Understand when the inequality sign must be reversed.
* Represent solutions on a number line.
* Write inequalities from word problems.
* Solve compound inequalities.

---

# 1. What Is an Inequality?

An **inequality** compares two quantities that are not necessarily equal.

Instead of using an equal sign:

$$
=
$$

inequalities use symbols such as:

$$
<,\quad >,\quad \le,\quad \ge
$$

For example:

$$
x>5
$$

means:

> $x$ is greater than 5.

---

# 2. Inequality Symbols

| Symbol | Meaning                  |
| ------ | ------------------------ |
| $<$    | less than                |
| $>$    | greater than             |
| $\le$  | less than or equal to    |
| $\ge$  | greater than or equal to |

---

## Examples

$$
x<7
$$

means:

> $x$ is less than 7.

$$
x>7
$$

means:

> $x$ is greater than 7.

$$
x\le7
$$

means:

> $x$ is less than or equal to 7.

$$
x\ge7
$$

means:

> $x$ is greater than or equal to 7.

---

# 3. Understanding the Symbols

The wider side of the inequality symbol faces the larger number.

For example:

$$
8>3
$$

The open side faces 8.

The pointed side faces 3.

A useful memory trick:

> The symbol looks like an arrow pointing toward the smaller number.

---

# 4. Inequalities vs. Equations

An equation usually has one solution.

Example:

$$
x+3=7
$$

$$
x=4
$$

An inequality often has many solutions.

Example:

$$
x+3>7
$$

$$
x>4
$$

The solutions include:

$$
5,\quad6,\quad7,\quad8,\quad\ldots
$$

---

# 5. Solving One-Step Inequalities

Solve inequalities using the same operations used for equations.

---

## Addition

Solve:

$$
x+4>10
$$

Subtract 4 from both sides:

$$
x>6
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{x>6}
$$

---

## Subtraction

Solve:

$$
x-5\le9
$$

Add 5 to both sides:

$$
\boxed{x\le14}
$$

---

## Multiplication by a Positive Number

Solve:

$$
3x>12
$$

Divide by 3:

$$
\boxed{x>4}
$$

The inequality sign does not change because we divided by a positive number.

---

# 6. The Important Rule: Multiplying or Dividing by a Negative

When multiplying or dividing both sides of an inequality by a negative number, **reverse the inequality sign**.

For example:

$$
-2x>8
$$

Divide by $-2$:

$$
\boxed{x<-4}
$$

The sign changes from:

$$
>
$$

to:

$$
<
$$

---

# 7. Why Does the Sign Reverse?

Consider:

$$
3<5
$$

Multiply both sides by $-1$:

$$
-3>-5
$$

The order changes because:

$$
-3
$$

is greater than:

$$
-5
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Multiplying or dividing by a negative reverses the inequality sign.}}
$$

---

# 8. Solving Two-Step Inequalities

Solve:

$$
3x+5>20
$$

### Step 1: Subtract 5

$$
3x>15
$$

### Step 2: Divide by 3

$$
\boxed{x>5}
$$

---

## Example with a Negative Coefficient

Solve:

$$
-4x+3\le19
$$

Subtract 3:

$$
-4x\le16
$$

Divide by $-4$.

Because we divide by a negative, reverse the sign:

$$
\boxed{x\ge-4}
$$

---

# 9. Solving Multi-Step Inequalities

Solve:

$$
3x+5-2x>12
$$

### Step 1: Combine like terms

$$
x+5>12
$$

### Step 2: Subtract 5

$$
\boxed{x>7}
$$

---

# 10. Solving Inequalities with Parentheses

Use the distributive property.

Solve:

$$
3(x+2)>18
$$

Distribute:

$$
3x+6>18
$$

Subtract 6:

$$
3x>12
$$

Divide by 3:

$$
\boxed{x>4}
$$

---

## Example

Solve:

$$
-2(x-3)\ge10
$$

Distribute:

$$
-2x+6\ge10
$$

Subtract 6:

$$
-2x\ge4
$$

Divide by $-2$ and reverse the sign:

$$
\boxed{x\le-2}
$$

---

# 11. Variables on Both Sides

Solve:

$$
5x+3>2x+15
$$

Subtract $2x$:

$$
3x+3>15
$$

Subtract 3:

$$
3x>12
$$

Divide by 3:

$$
\boxed{x>4}
$$

---

# 12. Inequalities with Fractions

Solve:

$$
\frac{x}{3}+2\le8
$$

Subtract 2:

$$
\frac{x}{3}\le6
$$

Multiply by 3:

$$
\boxed{x\le18}
$$

---

## Example

Solve:

$$
-\frac{x}{4}>3
$$

Multiply both sides by $-4$.

Because we multiply by a negative, reverse the sign:

$$
\boxed{x<-12}
$$

---

# 13. Inequalities with Decimals

Solve:

$$
0.5x+2>6
$$

Subtract 2:

$$
0.5x>4
$$

Divide by $0.5$:

$$
\boxed{x>8}
$$

---

# 14. Graphing Inequalities on a Number Line

There are two important types of points.

## Open Circle

Use an open circle for:

$$
<
$$

or:

$$
>
$$

The endpoint is not included.

Example:

$$
x>3
$$

Use an open circle at 3 and shade to the right.

---

## Closed Circle

Use a closed circle for:

$$
\le
$$

or:

$$
\ge
$$

The endpoint is included.

Example:

$$
x\ge3
$$

Use a closed circle at 3 and shade to the right.

---

# 15. Direction of the Shading

### Greater Than

$$
x>4
$$

Shade to the right.

### Less Than

$$
x<4
$$

Shade to the left.

---

# 16. Interval Notation

Inequalities can also be written using interval notation.

For:

$$
x>3
$$

the interval notation is:

$$
\boxed{(3,\infty)}
$$

For:

$$
x\ge3
$$

the interval notation is:

$$
\boxed{[3,\infty)}
$$

For:

$$
x<3
$$

the interval notation is:

$$
\boxed{(-\infty,3)}
$$

For:

$$
x\le3
$$

the interval notation is:

$$
\boxed{(-\infty,3]}
$$

Parentheses mean the endpoint is not included.

Brackets mean the endpoint is included.

---

# 17. Writing Inequalities from Words

### "Greater than"

> A number is greater than 5.

$$
\boxed{x>5}
$$

### "Less than"

> A number is less than 5.

$$
\boxed{x<5}
$$

### "At least"

> A number is at least 5.

$$
\boxed{x\ge5}
$$

At least means:

> 5 or more.

### "At most"

> A number is at most 5.

$$
\boxed{x\le5}
$$

At most means:

> 5 or less.

---

# 18. Common Word Translations

| Words        | Symbol |
| ------------ | ------ |
| greater than | $>$    |
| more than    | $>$    |
| less than    | $<$    |
| fewer than   | $<$    |
| at least     | $\ge$  |
| no less than | $\ge$  |
| at most      | $\le$  |
| no more than | $\le$  |
| maximum      | $\le$  |
| minimum      | $\ge$  |

# 19. Example: Budget Problem

You have $50 to spend. A book costs $12, and each notebook costs $4.

Let:

$$
n=\text{number of notebooks}
$$

The total cost must be at most $50$:

$$
12+4n\le50
$$

Subtract 12:

$$
4n\le38
$$

Divide by 4:

$$
n\le9.5
$$

Since you cannot buy half a notebook:

$$
\boxed{n\le9}
$$

You can buy at most 9 notebooks.

---

# 20. Example: Age Requirement

A person must be at least 18 years old to apply.

Let:

$$
a=\text{age}
$$

The inequality is:

$$
\boxed{a\ge18}
$$

---

# 21. Example: Speed Limit

A road has a speed limit of 65 miles per hour.

Let:

$$
s=\text{speed}
$$

The speed must be no more than 65:

$$
\boxed{s\le65}
$$

# 22. Compound Inequalities

A **compound inequality** contains two inequalities.

Example:

$$
3<x<8
$$

This means:

> $x$ is greater than 3 and less than 8.

The solution includes numbers between 3 and 8.

---

# 23. Solving Compound Inequalities

Solve:

$$
2<x+3\le8
$$

Subtract 3 from all three parts:

$$
2-3<x+3-3\le8-3
$$

Simplify:

$$
\boxed{-1<x\le5}
$$

---

# 24. Compound Inequalities with "And"

An "and" inequality requires both conditions to be true.

Example:

$$
x>2
$$

and:

$$
x<7
$$

Together:

$$
\boxed{2<x<7}
$$

---

# 25. Compound Inequalities with "Or"

An "or" inequality means either condition can be true.

Example:

$$
x<2
$$

or:

$$
x>7
$$

The solution includes values outside the interval from 2 to 7.

---

# 26. Solving an "Or" Inequality

Solve:

$$
x+3<5
$$

or:

$$
x-2>6
$$

Solve each separately.

First:

$$
x<2
$$

Second:

$$
x>8
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{x<2\text{ or }x>8}
$$

---

# 27. No Solution

Consider:

$$
x>5
$$

and:

$$
x<2
$$

No number can be both greater than 5 and less than 2.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{No solution}}
$$

---

# 28. All Real Numbers

Consider:

$$
x>2
$$

or:

$$
x\le5
$$

Every real number satisfies at least one of these conditions.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{All real numbers}}
$$

---

# 29. Absolute Value Inequalities

Absolute value represents distance from zero.

For example:

$$
|x|<3
$$

means:

> The distance of $x$ from zero is less than 3.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{-3<x<3}
$$

---

# 30. Absolute Value Less Than a Number

The rule is:

$$
|x|<a
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
-a<x<a
$$

Example:

$$
|x|<5
$$

becomes:

$$
\boxed{-5<x<5}
$$

---

# 31. Absolute Value Greater Than a Number

The rule is:

$$
|x|>a
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x<-a\text{ or }x>a
$$

Example:

$$
|x|>4
$$

becomes:

$$
\boxed{x<-4\text{ or }x>4}
$$

---

# 32. Solving an Absolute Value Inequality

Solve:

$$
|2x+1|\le5
$$

Rewrite as a compound inequality:

$$
-5\le2x+1\le5
$$

Subtract 1 from all parts:

$$
-6\le2x\le4
$$

Divide by 2:

$$
\boxed{-3\le x\le2}
$$

---

# 33. Common Mistakes

## Mistake 1: Forgetting to Reverse the Sign

Incorrect:

$$
-2x>8
$$

$$
x>-4
$$

Correct:

$$
\boxed{x<-4}
$$

---

## Mistake 2: Using an Open Circle for "Equal To"

For:

$$
x\ge4
$$

use a closed circle because 4 is included.

---

## Mistake 3: Confusing "At Least" and "At Most"

### At least 10

$$
\boxed{x\ge10}
$$

### At most 10

$$
\boxed{x\le10}
$$

---

## Mistake 4: Reversing the Sign When Adding or Subtracting

You only reverse the inequality sign when multiplying or dividing by a negative number.

For example:

$$
x-5>2
$$

Add 5:

$$
x>7
$$

The sign does not change.

---

# 34. A Reliable Strategy

When solving an inequality:

1. Simplify both sides.
2. Distribute if necessary.
3. Combine like terms.
4. Move variable terms to one side.
5. Move constants to the other side.
6. Isolate the variable.
7. Reverse the inequality sign if multiplying or dividing by a negative.
8. Graph the solution if needed.

# Summary

An inequality compares quantities.

The main symbols are:

$$
<,\quad>,\quad\le,\quad\ge
$$

To solve inequalities:

* Use the same steps as solving equations.
* Keep the inequality balanced.
* Reverse the sign when multiplying or dividing by a negative.
* Use open circles for $<$ and $>$.
* Use closed circles for $\le$ and $\ge$.

Important translations:

$$
\boxed{\text{at least}=\ge}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{at most}=\le}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{greater than}= >}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{less than}= <}
$$

The key idea is:

$$
\boxed{\text{An inequality represents a range of possible solutions, not usually just one value.}}
$$
