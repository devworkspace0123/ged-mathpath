# GED Math Lesson: Surface Area

## Goal

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

* Understand what surface area measures.
* Find the surface area of cubes and rectangular prisms.
* Find the surface area of cylinders, cones, and spheres.
* Identify missing or excluded surfaces.
* Solve GED-style surface-area problems.

---

# 1. What Is Surface Area?

**Surface area** is the total area of all the outside surfaces of a three-dimensional object.

Think of surface area as:

* The amount of wrapping paper needed to cover a box.
* The amount of paint needed to cover the outside of an object.
* The total area of all the faces and curved surfaces.

Surface area is measured in **square units**.

Examples:

$$
\text{in}^2,\quad \text{ft}^2,\quad \text{cm}^2,\quad \text{m}^2
$$

---

# 2. Surface Area vs. Volume

These concepts are different.

### Surface Area

Measures the **outside** of an object.

Example:

> How much wrapping paper is needed?

### Volume

Measures the **space inside** an object.

Example:

> How much water can the container hold?

### Memory Trick

**Cover the outside → Surface Area**

**Fill the inside → Volume**

---

# 3. Surface Area of a Cube

A cube has 6 identical square faces.

Each face has area:

$$
s^2
$$

Since there are 6 faces:

$$
SA=6s^2
$$

where $s$ is the side length.

### Example

A cube has a side length of 5 cm.

$$
SA=6(5)^2
$$

$$
SA=6(25)
$$

$$
SA=150
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{150\text{ cm}^2}
$$

---

# 4. Surface Area of a Rectangular Prism

A rectangular prism has three pairs of equal rectangular faces.

The formula is:

$$
SA=2lw+2lh+2wh
$$

where:

* $l$ = length
* $w$ = width
* $h$ = height

### Example

A box has:

* Length = 8 ft
* Width = 5 ft
* Height = 4 ft

$$
SA=2(8)(5)+2(8)(4)+2(5)(4)
$$

$$
SA=80+64+40
$$

$$
SA=184
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{184\text{ ft}^2}
$$

---

# 5. Surface Area of a Cylinder

A cylinder has:

* Two circular bases
* One curved surface

The formula is:

$$
SA=2\pi r^2+2\pi rh
$$

where:

* $r$ = radius
* $h$ = height

The two parts represent:

$$
2\pi r^2
$$

for the two circular bases, and:

$$
2\pi rh
$$

for the curved surface.

### Example

A cylinder has:

* Radius = 3 in
* Height = 8 in

Using:

$$
\pi\approx3.14
$$

$$
SA=2(3.14)(3)^2+2(3.14)(3)(8)
$$

$$
SA=56.52+150.72
$$

$$
SA=207.24
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{207.24\text{ in}^2}
$$

---

# 6. Surface Area of a Cone

A cone has:

* One circular base
* One curved surface

The formula for total surface area is:

$$
SA=\pi r^2+\pi rl
$$

This can also be written as:

$$
SA=\pi r(r+l)
$$

where:

* $r$ = radius
* $l$ = slant height

### Important

For the surface area of a cone, use the **slant height**, not the vertical height.

### Example

A cone has:

* Radius = 4 cm
* Slant height = 6 cm

$$
SA=\pi r(r+l)
$$

Using:

$$
\pi\approx3.14
$$

$$
SA=3.14(4)(4+6)
$$

$$
SA=3.14(4)(10)
$$

$$
SA=125.6
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{125.6\text{ cm}^2}
$$

---

# 7. Surface Area of a Sphere

A sphere has no flat faces.

Its surface area is:

$$
SA=4\pi r^2
$$

where $r$ is the radius.

### Example

A sphere has a radius of 5 m.

$$
SA=4(3.14)(5)^2
$$

$$
SA=4(3.14)(25)
$$

$$
SA=314
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{314\text{ m}^2}
$$

---

# 8. Open Objects

Not every object has all of its surfaces.

If a box has no top, do not include the top in the surface area.

### Example

A rectangular fish tank measures:

* Length = 20 in
* Width = 12 in
* Height = 10 in

The tank has an open top.

First find the surface area of a closed rectangular prism:

$$
SA=2lw+2lh+2wh
$$

$$
SA=2(20)(12)+2(20)(10)+2(12)(10)
$$

$$
SA=480+400+240
$$

$$
SA=1120
$$

The missing top has area:

$$
20(12)=240
$$

Subtract:

$$
1120-240=880
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{880\text{ in}^2}
$$

---

# 9. GED-Style Example: Cube

A gift box is shaped like a cube.

Each side measures 9 inches.

How much wrapping paper is needed to completely cover the box?

$$
SA=6s^2
$$

$$
SA=6(9)^2
$$

$$
SA=6(81)
$$

$$
SA=486
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{486\text{ in}^2}
$$

---

# 10. GED-Style Example: Cylinder

A cylindrical can has:

* Radius = 5 cm
* Height = 12 cm

Find the total surface area.

$$
SA=2\pi r^2+2\pi rh
$$

Using:

$$
\pi\approx3.14
$$

$$
SA=2(3.14)(5)^2+2(3.14)(5)(12)
$$

$$
SA=157+376.8
$$

$$
SA=533.8
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{533.8\text{ cm}^2}
$$

---

# 11. GED-Style Example: Sphere

A spherical ornament has a radius of 7 cm.

Find its surface area.

$$
SA=4\pi r^2
$$

$$
SA=4(3.14)(7)^2
$$

$$
SA=4(3.14)(49)
$$

$$
SA=615.44
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{615.44\text{ cm}^2}
$$

# Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Confusing Area and Surface Area

A flat shape has area.

A 3D object has surface area.

---

### Mistake 2: Using Linear Units

Surface area must use square units.

Correct:

$$
\text{cm}^2
$$

Incorrect:

$$
\text{cm}
$$

---

### Mistake 3: Including a Missing Surface

If an object is open, do not count the missing face.

---

### Mistake 4: Using the Wrong Height for a Cone

For cone surface area, the formula uses the **slant height**.

## Memory Trick

**Surface Area = Cover the Outside**

Ask yourself:

> What surfaces are actually exposed?

Then calculate the area of each exposed surface and add them together.
