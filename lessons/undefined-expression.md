# GED Math Lesson: Undefined Expressions

## Goal

Learn how to recognize when a mathematical expression has no defined value, especially when a denominator becomes zero.

---

# 1. What Does Undefined Mean?

An expression is **undefined** when mathematics does not assign it a valid numerical value.

The most common GED situation occurs when:

$$
\boxed{\text{Division by zero}}
$$

---

# 2. The Denominator Cannot Equal Zero

A fraction has:

* A numerator on top.
* A denominator on the bottom.

$$
\frac{\text{numerator}}{\text{denominator}}
$$

The denominator cannot equal $0$.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\frac{a}{0}\text{ is undefined}}
$$

### Examples

$$
\frac{8}{0}
$$

is undefined.

$$
\frac{-25}{0}
$$

is undefined.

$$
\frac{100}{0}
$$

is undefined.

---

# 3. Why Can't We Divide by Zero?

Division asks:

> What number multiplied by the divisor gives the dividend?

For example:

$$
12\div4=3
$$

because:

$$
3\times4=12
$$

Now consider:

$$
12\div0
$$

This asks:

> What number multiplied by $0$ equals $12$?

But:

$$
0\times1=0
$$

$$
0\times100=0
$$

$$
0\times1{,}000{,}000=0
$$

No number multiplied by $0$ equals $12$.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{12\div0\text{ is undefined}}
$$

---

# 4. Zero in the Numerator Is Different

A numerator can equal zero as long as the denominator is not zero.

### Example

$$
\frac{0}{5}=0
$$

because:

$$
0\div5=0
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\frac{0}{a}=0\quad\text{when }a\ne0}
$$

---

### Compare

$$
\frac{0}{5}=0
$$

but:

$$
\frac{5}{0}
$$

is undefined.

### Important

**Top can be zero. Bottom cannot be zero.**

---

# 5. Expressions with Variables

Sometimes a variable appears in the denominator.

### Example

Evaluate:

$$
\frac{8}{x}
$$

when:

$$
x=2
$$

Substitute:

$$
\frac{8}{2}=4
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{4}
$$

---

### Example

Evaluate:

$$
\frac{8}{x}
$$

when:

$$
x=0
$$

Substitute:

$$
\frac{8}{0}
$$

Because the denominator is zero:

$$
\boxed{\text{Undefined}}
$$

---

# 6. Finding the Value That Makes an Expression Undefined

Set the denominator equal to zero.

### Example

For what value of $x$ is this expression undefined?

$$
\frac{7}{x-5}
$$

The denominator is:

$$
x-5
$$

Set it equal to zero:

$$
x-5=0
$$

Solve:

$$
x=5
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{x=5}
$$

makes the expression undefined.

---

### Example

For what value of $x$ is this expression undefined?

$$
\frac{3x+2}{x+9}
$$

Set the denominator equal to zero:

$$
x+9=0
$$

$$
x=-9
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{x=-9}
$$

---

# 7. Expressions That Look Complicated

Always simplify the denominator first.

### Example

Evaluate:

$$
\frac{18}{6-6}
$$

First:

$$
6-6=0
$$

So the expression becomes:

$$
\frac{18}{0}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Undefined}}
$$

---

### Example

Evaluate:

$$
\frac{0}{9-4}
$$

First:

$$
9-4=5
$$

Now:

$$
\frac{0}{5}=0
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{0}
$$

---

# 8. Undefined Expressions and Graphs

Some functions have values that are not allowed.

For example:

$$
f(x)=\frac{1}{x-3}
$$

The denominator cannot be zero.

Set:

$$
x-3=0
$$

$$
x=3
$$

Therefore, the function is undefined at:

$$
\boxed{x=3}
$$

This means $x=3$ is not allowed in the expression.

---

# Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Thinking $0\div5$ Is Undefined

It is not.

$$
0\div5=0
$$

---

### Mistake 2: Thinking $5\div0=0$

It is not.

$$
\frac{5}{0}
$$

is undefined.

---

### Mistake 3: Forgetting to Simplify the Denominator

For:

$$
\frac{10}{x-4}
$$

when $x=4$:

$$
x-4=0
$$

so the expression is undefined.

---

# GED Tips

* Always check the denominator.
* If the denominator equals $0$, the expression is undefined.
* A numerator of $0$ is allowed if the denominator is not $0$.
* To find where a rational expression is undefined, set the denominator equal to $0$.
* Simplify expressions before deciding whether they are undefined.

## Memory Trick

Think of a fraction as a house:

* **Top = numerator:** can be $0$.
* **Bottom = denominator:** can never be $0$.

Remember:

$$
\boxed{\text{Top can be zero. Bottom cannot be zero.}}
$$
