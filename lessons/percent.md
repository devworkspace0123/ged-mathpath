# GED Math Lesson: Percents

## Learning Goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

* Convert between fractions, decimals, and percents.
* Find a percent of a number.
* Find what percent one number is of another.
* Find the original whole when a part and percent are known.
* Calculate percent increase and decrease.
* Solve discount and sales tax problems.
* Calculate tips, commissions, and markups.
* Calculate simple interest.
* Solve multi-step GED-style percent problems.

---

# 1. What Is a Percent?

A **percent** means **per hundred**.

The symbol for percent is:

$$
\%
$$

For example:

$$
35\%=\frac{35}{100}
$$

As a decimal:

$$
35\%=0.35
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{35\%=\frac{35}{100}=0.35}
$$

---

# 2. Converting Between Percents, Decimals, and Fractions

These three forms can represent the same value:

$$
\boxed{\text{Fraction}\leftrightarrow\text{Decimal}\leftrightarrow\text{Percent}}
$$

---

## Percent to Decimal

Divide by 100.

Move the decimal point two places to the left.

### Examples

$$
75\%=0.75
$$

$$
8\%=0.08
$$

$$
125\%=1.25
$$

---

## Decimal to Percent

Multiply by 100.

Move the decimal point two places to the right.

### Examples

$$
0.42=42\%
$$

$$
0.08=8\%
$$

$$
1.5=150\%
$$

---

## Fraction to Percent

Convert the fraction to a decimal, then multiply by 100.

### Example

$$
\frac{3}{4}
$$

Convert to a decimal:

$$
\frac{3}{4}=0.75
$$

Convert to a percent:

$$
0.75=75\%
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\frac{3}{4}=0.75=75\%}
$$

---

## Percent to Fraction

Write the percent over 100 and simplify.

### Example

$$
60\%=\frac{60}{100}
$$

Simplify:

$$
\frac{60}{100}=\frac{3}{5}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{60\%=\frac{3}{5}}
$$

---

# 3. The Percent Relationship

Most percent problems involve three quantities:

$$
\boxed{\text{Part}=\text{Percent}\times\text{Whole}}
$$

This relationship can be rearranged:

$$
\boxed{\text{Percent}=\frac{\text{Part}}{\text{Whole}}}
$$

and:

$$
\boxed{\text{Whole}=\frac{\text{Part}}{\text{Percent}}}
$$

Always convert the percent to a decimal before using these formulas.

---

# 4. Finding the Percent of a Number

## Formula

$$
\text{Part}=\text{Percent}\times\text{Whole}
$$

### Example

Find 25% of 80.

Convert the percent:

$$
25\%=0.25
$$

Multiply:

$$
0.25\times80=20
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{20}
$$

---

# 5. Finding What Percent One Number Is of Another

## Formula

$$
\text{Percent}
=
\frac{\text{Part}}{\text{Whole}}\times100
$$

### Example

18 out of 24 students passed.

Here:

* Part = 18
* Whole = 24

$$
\frac{18}{24}=0.75
$$

Convert to a percent:

$$
0.75\times100=75\%
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{75\%}
$$

---

# 6. Finding the Whole

## Formula

$$
\text{Whole}
=
\frac{\text{Part}}{\text{Percent}}
$$

Convert the percent to a decimal first.

### Example

30 is 20% of what number?

Convert:

$$
20\%=0.20
$$

Now:

$$
\text{Whole}=\frac{30}{0.20}
$$

$$
\text{Whole}=150
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{150}
$$

---

# 7. Percent Increase

A percent increase compares how much something increased with the original amount.

## Formula

$$
\text{Percent Increase}
=
\frac{\text{Increase}}{\text{Original}}\times100
$$

First find:

$$
\text{Increase}
=
\text{New Amount}-\text{Original Amount}
$$

---

## Example

The price of a bicycle increases from $200 to $250.

### Step 1: Find the Increase

$$
250-200=50
$$

### Step 2: Divide by the Original Amount

$$
\frac{50}{200}=0.25
$$

### Step 3: Convert to a Percent

$$
0.25\times100=25\%
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{25\%\text{ increase}}
$$

---

# 8. Percent Decrease

## Formula

$$
\text{Percent Decrease}
=
\frac{\text{Decrease}}{\text{Original}}\times100
$$

First find:

$$
\text{Decrease}
=
\text{Original Amount}-\text{New Amount}
$$

---

## Example

A jacket's price drops from $120 to $90.

### Step 1: Find the Decrease

$$
120-90=30
$$

### Step 2: Divide by the Original Amount

$$
\frac{30}{120}=0.25
$$

### Step 3: Convert to a Percent

$$
0.25\times100=25\%
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{25\%\text{ decrease}}
$$

---

# 9. Discounts

A discount reduces the original price.

## Step 1: Find the Discount

$$
\text{Discount}
=
\text{Discount Rate}\times\text{Original Price}
$$

## Step 2: Subtract the Discount

$$
\text{Sale Price}
=
\text{Original Price}-\text{Discount}
$$

---

## Example

A jacket costs $80 and is 25% off.

### Find the Discount

$$
0.25\times80=20
$$

### Find the Sale Price

$$
80-20=60
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\$60}
$$

---

# 10. Sales Tax

Sales tax increases the purchase price.

## Step 1: Find the Tax

$$
\text{Tax}
=
\text{Tax Rate}\times\text{Price}
$$

## Step 2: Add the Tax

$$
\text{Total}
=
\text{Price}+\text{Tax}
$$

---

## Example

A laptop costs $600 and sales tax is 8%.

### Tax

$$
0.08\times600=48
$$

### Total

$$
600+48=648
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\$648}
$$

---

# 11. Tips

A tip is calculated as a percentage of a bill.

### Example

A restaurant bill is $45.

You leave a 20% tip.

### Tip

$$
0.20\times45=9
$$

### Total Payment

$$
45+9=54
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\$54}
$$

---

# 12. Commission

A commission is money earned as a percentage of sales.

## Formula

$$
\text{Commission}
=
\text{Commission Rate}\times\text{Sales}
$$

### Example

A salesperson earns a 6% commission on $8,000 in sales.

$$
0.06\times8000=480
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\$480}
$$

---

# 13. Markup

A markup increases the selling price above the original cost.

## Step 1: Find the Markup

$$
\text{Markup}
=
\text{Markup Rate}\times\text{Cost}
$$

## Step 2: Find the Selling Price

$$
\text{Selling Price}
=
\text{Cost}+\text{Markup}
$$

---

## Example

A store buys a chair for $120 and marks it up by 30%.

### Markup

$$
0.30\times120=36
$$

### Selling Price

$$
120+36=156
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\$156}
$$

---

# 14. Simple Interest

Simple interest is calculated using the original amount of money.

The original amount is called the **principal**.

## Formula

$$
I=Prt
$$

where:

* $I$ = interest
* $P$ = principal
* $r$ = annual interest rate as a decimal
* $t$ = time in years

---

## Example

Maria deposits $500 into a savings account earning 4% simple interest per year.

How much interest will she earn after 3 years?

### Convert the Rate

$$
4\%=0.04
$$

### Use the Formula

$$
I=Prt
$$

$$
I=500(0.04)(3)
$$

$$
I=60
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\$60\text{ interest}}
$$

---

## Finding the Total Amount

The total amount is:

$$
\text{Total}=\text{Principal}+\text{Interest}
$$

Therefore:

$$
500+60=560
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{\$560}
$$

---

# 15. GED-Style Mixed Problem

A bicycle originally costs $450.

The store offers a 20% discount.

After the discount, an 8% sales tax is added.

What is the final price?

---

## Step 1: Find the Discount

$$
0.20\times450=90
$$

Sale price:

$$
450-90=360
$$

---

## Step 2: Find the Sales Tax

$$
0.08\times360=28.80
$$

---

## Step 3: Find the Final Price

$$
360+28.80=388.80
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\$388.80}
$$

# 16. Percent Formula Reference

| Problem Type     | Formula                                            |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Find the part    | $\text{Part}=\text{Percent}\times\text{Whole}$     |
| Find the percent | $\text{Percent}=\frac{\text{Part}}{\text{Whole}}$  |
| Find the whole   | $\text{Whole}=\frac{\text{Part}}{\text{Percent}}$  |
| Percent increase | $\frac{\text{Increase}}{\text{Original}}\times100$ |
| Percent decrease | $\frac{\text{Decrease}}{\text{Original}}\times100$ |
| Simple interest  | $I=Prt$                                            |

---

# GED Tips

* Convert percentages to decimals before multiplying.
* Percent means **per hundred**.
* Percent increase and decrease are based on the **original amount**.
* A discount is subtracted.
* Sales tax is added.
* Tips are added to the bill.
* Commission is a percentage of sales.
* Markup is added to the cost.
* Simple interest uses:

$$
I=Prt
$$

* Always pay attention to whether the question asks for:

  * The part.
  * The percent.
  * The whole.

---

# Memory Trick

## Part

$$
\boxed{\text{Part}=\text{Percent}\times\text{Whole}}
$$

## Percent

$$
\boxed{\text{Percent}=\frac{\text{Part}}{\text{Whole}}}
$$

## Whole

$$
\boxed{\text{Whole}=\frac{\text{Part}}{\text{Percent}}}
$$

Think:

> **Part = Percent × Whole**

For real-world problems:

* **Discount → Subtract**
* **Tax → Add**
* **Tip → Add**
* **Commission → Multiply**
* **Markup → Add**
* **Interest → Use $I=Prt$**
