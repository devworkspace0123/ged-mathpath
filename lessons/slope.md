# Slope

## Learning Goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

* Understand what slope represents.
* Calculate slope using two points.
* Find slope from a graph.
* Find slope from a table.
* Find slope from an equation.
* Understand positive, negative, zero, and undefined slope.
* Use the slope formula.
* Interpret slope as a rate of change.
* Write linear equations using slope.
* Solve real-world problems involving slope.

---

# 1. What Is Slope?

**Slope** measures the steepness and direction of a line.

It describes how much the $y$-value changes when the $x$-value changes.

The formula is:

$$
\boxed{m=\frac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}}}
$$

or:

$$
\boxed{m=\frac{\text{change in }y}{\text{change in }x}}
$$

The letter $m$ represents slope.

---

# 2. The Slope Formula

Given two points:

$$
(x_1,y_1)
$$

and:

$$
(x_2,y_2)
$$

the slope is:

$$
\boxed{m=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}}
$$

The order must be consistent.

If you subtract:

$$
y_2-y_1
$$

in the numerator, you must use:

$$
x_2-x_1
$$

in the denominator.

---

# 3. Finding Slope from Two Points

Find the slope of the line through:

$$
(2,3)
$$

and:

$$
(5,9)
$$

Use:

$$
m=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}
$$

Substitute:

$$
m=\frac{9-3}{5-2}
$$

Simplify:

$$
m=\frac{6}{3}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{m=2}
$$

---

# 4. Rise Over Run

Suppose a line rises 6 units and runs 3 units.

Then:

$$
m=\frac{6}{3}
$$

$$
\boxed{m=2}
$$

This means:

> For every 1 unit the line moves to the right, it rises 2 units.

---

# 5. Positive Slope

A line has a positive slope when it rises from left to right.

Example:

$$
m=3
$$

The line:

* Goes upward as $x$ increases.
* Has a positive rate of change.

---

# 6. Negative Slope

A line has a negative slope when it falls from left to right.

Example:

$$
m=-2
$$

The line:

* Goes downward as $x$ increases.
* Has a negative rate of change.

---

# 7. Zero Slope

A horizontal line has a slope of:

$$
\boxed{0}
$$

Example:

$$
y=5
$$

The $y$-value never changes.

Therefore:

$$
m=\frac{\text{change in }y}{\text{change in }x}
$$

$$
m=\frac{0}{\text{number}}=0
$$

---

# 8. Undefined Slope

A vertical line has an undefined slope.

For example:

$$
x=4
$$

The change in $x$ is zero.

Therefore:

$$
m=\frac{\text{change in }y}{0}
$$

Division by zero is undefined.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Vertical line}=\text{undefined slope}}
$$

---

# 9. The Four Types of Slope

| Type      | Direction           | Slope     |
| --------- | ------------------- | --------- |
| Positive  | Rises left to right | $m>0$     |
| Negative  | Falls left to right | $m<0$     |
| Zero      | Horizontal          | $m=0$     |
| Undefined | Vertical            | Undefined |

---

# 10. Finding Slope from a Graph

To find the slope from a graph:

1. Choose two points on the line.
2. Count the vertical change.
3. Count the horizontal change.
4. Use:

$$
\boxed{m=\frac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}}}
$$

---

## Example

Suppose a line moves:

* Up 4 units.
* Right 2 units.

Then:

$$
m=\frac{4}{2}
$$

$$
\boxed{m=2}
$$

---

# 11. Negative Slope from a Graph

Suppose a line:

* Falls 3 units.
* Moves right 1 unit.

Then:

$$
\boxed{m=-3}
$$

The negative sign indicates that the line decreases from left to right.

---

# 12. Finding Slope from a Table

Consider:

| $x$ | $y$ |
| --: | --: |
|   1 |   4 |
|   2 |   7 |
|   3 |  10 |
|   4 |  13 |

When $x$ increases by 1:

$$
\Delta x=1
$$

When $y$ increases by 3:

$$
\Delta y=3
$$

Therefore:

$$
m=\frac{3}{1}
$$

$$
\boxed{m=3}
$$

---

# 13. Using Two Rows from a Table

Use:

$$
m=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}
$$

For the points:

$$
(1,4)
$$

and:

$$
(3,10)
$$

we get:

$$
m=\frac{10-4}{3-1}
$$

$$
m=\frac{6}{2}
$$

$$
\boxed{m=3}
$$

---

# 14. Finding Slope from an Equation

The slope-intercept form is:

$$
\boxed{y=mx+b}
$$

The coefficient of $x$ is the slope.

---

## Example

$$
y=4x+7
$$

The slope is:

$$
\boxed{m=4}
$$

---

## Example

$$
y=-2x+5
$$

The slope is:

$$
\boxed{m=-2}
$$

---

# 15. Slope When the Equation Is Not in Slope-Intercept Form

Consider:

$$
2x+3y=12
$$

Solve for $y$.

Subtract $2x$:

$$
3y=-2x+12
$$

Divide by 3:

$$
y=-\frac{2}{3}x+4
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{m=-\frac{2}{3}}
$$

---

# 16. Slope from a Linear Function

If:

$$
f(x)=5x-2
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{m=5}
$$

The function increases by 5 for every increase of 1 in $x$.

---

# 17. Slope as Rate of Change

Slope represents a rate of change.

For example:

$$
m=60
$$

could mean:

> The value increases by 60 units for every 1 unit of time.

---

# 18. Real-World Example: Pay

Suppose a worker earns:

* $100 after 2 hours.
* $250 after 5 hours.

The rate of change is:

$$
m=\frac{250-100}{5-2}
$$

$$
m=\frac{150}{3}
$$

$$
\boxed{m=50}
$$

The worker earns:

$$
\boxed{\$50\text{ per hour}}
$$

---

# 19. Real-World Example: Distance

A car travels:

* 100 miles in 2 hours.
* 250 miles in 5 hours.

The rate is:

$$
m=\frac{250-100}{5-2}
$$

$$
m=50
$$

The car travels:

$$
\boxed{50\text{ miles per hour}}
$$

---

# 20. Slope and the $y$-Intercept

The slope-intercept form is:

$$
\boxed{y=mx+b}
$$

where:

* $m$ = slope
* $b$ = $y$-intercept

Example:

$$
y=3x+5
$$

Slope:

$$
\boxed{3}
$$

$y$-intercept:

$$
\boxed{5}
$$

---

# 21. Writing an Equation from Slope and Intercept

Suppose:

$$
m=4
$$

and:

$$
b=-2
$$

Use:

$$
y=mx+b
$$

Substitute:

$$
\boxed{y=4x-2}
$$

---

# 22. Writing an Equation from a Point and Slope

Suppose:

$$
m=3
$$

and the line passes through:

$$
(2,7)
$$

Start with:

$$
y=3x+b
$$

Substitute the point:

$$
7=3(2)+b
$$

$$
7=6+b
$$

Therefore:

$$
b=1
$$

The equation is:

$$
\boxed{y=3x+1}
$$

---

# 23. Point-Slope Form

The point-slope form is:

$$
\boxed{y-y_1=m(x-x_1)}
$$

Use it when you know:

* A point $(x_1,y_1)$.
* The slope $m$.

---

## Example

Find the equation of a line with slope 2 through:

$$
(3,5)
$$

Use:

$$
y-y_1=m(x-x_1)
$$

Substitute:

$$
y-5=2(x-3)
$$

This is the equation in point-slope form.

Simplify:

$$
y-5=2x-6
$$

Add 5:

$$
\boxed{y=2x-1}
$$

---

# 24. Parallel Lines

Parallel lines have the same slope.

For example:

$$
y=2x+3
$$

and:

$$
y=2x-7
$$

Both have:

$$
\boxed{m=2}
$$

Therefore, they are parallel.

---

# 25. Perpendicular Lines

Perpendicular lines meet at a 90-degree angle.

Their slopes are negative reciprocals.

If one slope is:

$$
m=2
$$

the perpendicular slope is:

$$
\boxed{-\frac{1}{2}}
$$

If one slope is:

$$
m=-\frac{3}{4}
$$

the perpendicular slope is:

$$
\boxed{\frac{4}{3}}
$$

---

# 26. Comparing Slopes

Consider:

$$
y=5x+1
$$

and:

$$
y=2x+7
$$

The first line has a greater slope:

$$
5>2
$$

Therefore, the first line increases faster.

---

# 27. Slope and Linear Relationships

A relationship is linear when the rate of change is constant.

For example:

| $x$ | $y$ |
| --: | --: |
|   1 |   5 |
|   2 |   8 |
|   3 |  11 |
|   4 |  14 |

The $y$-values increase by 3 each time.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{The slope is constant and the relationship is linear.}}
$$

---

# 28. Finding Slope from a Word Problem

A gym membership costs $20 to join and $15 per month.

The cost function is:

$$
C=15m+20
$$

The slope is:

$$
\boxed{15}
$$

This means the cost increases by:

$$
\boxed{\$15\text{ per month}}
$$

The $y$-intercept is:

$$
\boxed{20}
$$

This represents the initial membership fee.

---

# 29. Slope in a Scatter Plot

A scatter plot may show a trend.

If the points generally rise from left to right, the relationship has a:

$$
\boxed{\text{positive association}}
$$

If the points generally fall from left to right, the relationship has a:

$$
\boxed{\text{negative association}}
$$

If there is no clear pattern, the relationship may have little or no association.

---

# 30. Common Mistakes

## Mistake 1: Reversing Only One Difference

Incorrect:

$$
m=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_1-x_2}
$$

The subtraction order must match.

Correct:

$$
\boxed{m=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}}
$$

---

## Mistake 2: Forgetting the Negative Sign

If a line decreases from left to right, its slope is negative.

---

## Mistake 3: Confusing Slope and $y$-Intercept

In:

$$
y=4x+7
$$

the slope is:

$$
\boxed{4}
$$

and the $y$-intercept is:

$$
\boxed{7}
$$

---

## Mistake 4: Calling a Vertical Slope Zero

A horizontal line has slope 0.

A vertical line has an undefined slope.

---

# 31. A Reliable Strategy

When finding slope:

1. Identify two points.
2. Write the slope formula.
3. Subtract the $y$-values.
4. Subtract the corresponding $x$-values.
5. Simplify the fraction.
6. Interpret the result in context.

# Summary

Slope measures the rate of change of a line.

The main formula is:

$$
\boxed{m=\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}}
$$

or:

$$
\boxed{m=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}}
$$

In:

$$
\boxed{y=mx+b}
$$

* $m$ is the slope.
* $b$ is the $y$-intercept.

The four types of slope are:

$$
\boxed{\text{Positive}}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Negative}}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Zero}}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Undefined}}
$$

The key idea is:

$$
\boxed{\text{Slope tells you how much }y\text{ changes when }x\text{ changes.}}
$$
