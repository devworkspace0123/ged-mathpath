# GED Math Lesson: Unit Rates

## Goal

Learn how to calculate **unit rates**, compare rates, and solve GED problems involving prices, speed, wages, production, fuel efficiency, and other real-world situations.

---

# 1. What Is a Rate?

A **rate** compares two quantities with different units.

Examples:

* miles per hour
* dollars per pound
* pages per minute
* dollars per hour
* miles per gallon

### Example

A car travels $180$ miles in $3$ hours.

The rate is:

$$
\frac{180\text{ miles}}{3\text{ hours}}
$$

---

# 2. What Is a Unit Rate?

A **unit rate** tells how much there is for **1 unit**.

The word **per** usually means division.

### Formula

$$
\boxed{
\text{Unit Rate}
=
\frac{\text{Total Quantity}}{\text{Number of Units}}
}
$$

### Example

A car travels $180$ miles in $3$ hours.

$$
180\div3=60
$$

The unit rate is:

$$
\boxed{60\text{ miles per hour}}
$$

This means the car travels $60$ miles in $1$ hour.

---

# 3. Cost Per Item

### Example

Five notebooks cost $15$ dollars.

What is the cost per notebook?

$$
15\div5=3
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{3\text{ dollars per notebook}}
$$

---

### Example

Eight bottles cost $12$ dollars.

$$
12\div8=1.50
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{1.50\text{ dollars per bottle}}
$$

---

# 4. Finding Speed

Speed is a unit rate.

### Formula

$$
\boxed{
\text{Speed}
=
\frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Time}}
}
$$

### Example

A cyclist travels $72$ miles in $4$ hours.

$$
72\div4=18
$$

The average speed is:

$$
\boxed{18\text{ miles per hour}}
$$

---

# 5. Finding an Hourly Wage

### Example

Maria earns $126$ dollars for working $9$ hours.

$$
126\div9=14
$$

Her hourly wage is:

$$
\boxed{14\text{ dollars per hour}}
$$

---

# 6. Comparing Unit Rates

When comparing prices, calculate the cost for one item or one unit.

### Example

Store A sells $8$ apples for $12$ dollars.

Store B sells $10$ apples for $14$ dollars.

### Store A

$$
12\div8=1.50
$$

Store A costs $1.50$ dollars per apple.

### Store B

$$
14\div10=1.40
$$

Store B costs $1.40$ dollars per apple.

Since:

$$
1.40<1.50
$$

Store B offers the better price.

$$
\boxed{\text{Store B}}
$$

---

# 7. Unit Rates and Fractions

You may need to divide a fraction by a whole number.

### Example

A recipe uses:

$$
\frac{3}{4}
$$

cup of sugar for $3$ batches.

For one batch:

$$
\frac{3}{4}\div3
$$

Rewrite $3$ as:

$$
\frac{3}{1}
$$

Then multiply by the reciprocal:

$$
\frac{3}{4}\times\frac{1}{3}
$$

$$
=\frac{3}{12}
$$

$$
=\frac{1}{4}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\frac{1}{4}\text{ cup per batch}}
$$

---

# 8. Unit Rate and Fuel Efficiency

Fuel efficiency is often measured in miles per gallon.

### Example

A car travels $360$ miles using $12$ gallons of gasoline.

$$
360\div12=30
$$

The car gets:

$$
\boxed{30\text{ miles per gallon}}
$$

---

# 9. Unit Rate and Production

### Example

A machine produces $420$ parts in $7$ hours.

$$
420\div7=60
$$

The machine produces:

$$
\boxed{60\text{ parts per hour}}
$$

---

# 10. Unit Rates and Proportions

Once you know the unit rate, you can find any quantity.

### Example

A car travels $60$ miles per hour.

How far will it travel in $4.5$ hours?

$$
60\times4.5=270
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{270\text{ miles}}
$$

---

# 11. GED-Style Example

A store sells $6$ pounds of oranges for $18$ dollars.

What is the cost per pound?

$$
18\div6=3
$$

Answer:

$$
\boxed{3\text{ dollars per pound}}
$$

---

# Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Dividing in the Wrong Direction

If the question asks for dollars per item:

$$
\frac{\text{dollars}}{\text{items}}
$$

not:

$$
\frac{\text{items}}{\text{dollars}}
$$

---

### Mistake 2: Forgetting the Units

A numerical answer alone is incomplete.

For example:

$$
60
$$

should be:

$$
\boxed{60\text{ miles per hour}}
$$

---

### Mistake 3: Comparing Totals Instead of Unit Rates

A larger package is not always the better deal.

Always compare:

$$
\text{cost per one unit}
$$

---

# GED Tips

* **Unit rate means per 1.**
* The word **per** usually means divide.
* To find a unit rate:

$$
\boxed{\text{Total}\div\text{Number of Units}}
$$

* Always include the units.
* For the best price, choose the lower cost per unit.
* For speed, wages, and production, calculate the amount per unit of time.

## Memory Trick

**Unit = One**

Ask:

> How much for one?

* dollars per pound
* miles per hour
* pages per minute
* dollars per hour

If you can find the amount for **one**, you have found the unit rate.
