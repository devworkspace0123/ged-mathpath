# Writing Algebraic Expressions from Problem Situations

## Learning Goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

* Translate words into mathematical expressions.
* Identify unknown quantities and represent them with variables.
* Recognize keywords that indicate addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division.
* Write algebraic expressions from real-world situations.
* Translate word phrases into algebraic symbols.
* Avoid common mistakes when translating expressions.

---

# 1. What Is an Algebraic Expression?

An **algebraic expression** is a combination of numbers, variables, and mathematical operations.

Examples:

$$
x+5
$$

$$
3x-7
$$

$$
\frac{x}{4}+2
$$

$$
5(n+3)
$$

An expression does **not** contain an equal sign.

For example:

$$
3x+5
$$

is an expression.

However:

$$
3x+5=20
$$

is an equation because it contains an equal sign.

---

# 2. What Is a Variable?

A **variable** is a letter or symbol that represents an unknown or changing quantity.

For example, if you do not know the number of books a student has, you might represent the number of books with:

$$
b
$$

If one more book is added, the expression becomes:

$$
b+1
$$

If the student has three times as many books, the expression becomes:

$$
3b
$$

---

# 3. Translating Words into Symbols

Many word problems ask you to translate a verbal phrase into an algebraic expression.

For example:

> Five more than a number

Let the unknown number be $x$.

"Five more than" means add $5$:

$$
\boxed{x+5}
$$

| ------------ | ------ |

| more than    | $+$    |
| increased by | $+$    |
| greater than | $+$    |

## Examples

### A Number Plus 7

Let the number be $x$:

$$
\boxed{x+7}
$$

### 10 More Than a Number

$$
\boxed{x+10}
$$

### A Number Increased by 4

$$

---

# 6. Pay Attention to Word Order

Word order is especially important in subtraction.

### "5 Less Than a Number"

This means:

$$
\boxed{x-5}
$$

But:

### "A Number Less Than 5"

This means:

$$
\boxed{5-x}
$$

The phrase **"less than" reverses the order** of the numbers.

| ------------- | -------------- |
| product       | $\times$       |
| times         | $\times$       |
| twice         | $2\times$      |
| three times   | $3\times$      |
| of            | often $\times$ |
| multiplied by | $\times$       |

## Examples

### Three Times a Number

$$
\boxed{3x}
$$

### Twice a Number

$$
\boxed{2x}
$$

### The Product of 5 and a Number

$$
\boxed{5x}
$$

### Four Times the Sum of a Number and 3

The sum of a number and $3$ is:

$$
x+3
$$

Four times the entire sum is:

$$
\boxed{4(x+3)}
$$

# 10. Using Parentheses

Parentheses are important when a number applies to an entire expression.

Consider:

> Five times the sum of a number and 2.

First, identify the sum:

$$
x+2
$$

Then multiply the entire sum by $5$:

$$
\boxed{5(x+2)}
$$

This is different from:

$$
5x+2
$$

The expression $5x+2$ means only $x$ is multiplied by $5$.

# 11. Translating Real-World Situations

Algebraic expressions are useful for representing real situations.

## Example 1: Ticket Cost

A movie ticket costs $12$. You buy $t$ tickets.

The total cost is:

$$
\boxed{12t}
$$

---

## Example 2: Shopping

You have $50 and spend $x$ dollars.

The amount of money remaining is:

$$
\boxed{50-x}
$$

---

## Example 3: Age

Sara is $x$ years old. Her brother is 4 years older.

Her brother's age is:

$$
\boxed{x+4}
$$

---

## Example 4: Perimeter

A rectangle has a length of $x+3$ and a width of $x$.

The perimeter is:

$$
P=2(x+3)+2x
$$

This expression can be simplified:

$$
P=2x+6+2x
$$

$$
\boxed{P=4x+6}
$$

# 12. Writing Expressions from Word Problems

When translating a situation, follow these steps:

### Step 1: Identify the Unknown

Choose a variable.

Example:

> Let $x$ represent the number of hours.

### Step 2: Identify the Operations

Look for words such as:

* more
* less
* times
* per
* total
* difference

### Step 3: Translate the Words

Convert each part into symbols.

### Step 4: Use Parentheses When Necessary

Make sure the order of operations matches the situation.

---

# 13. Example: A Phone Plan

A phone company charges a monthly fee of $25 plus $10 for each gigabyte of data used.

Let $g$ represent the number of gigabytes used.

The monthly cost is:

$$
\boxed{25+10g}
$$

The $25$ is a fixed cost.

The $10g$ represents the cost based on data usage.

---

# 14. Example: A Taxi Ride

A taxi charges a $5 starting fee and $2 for every mile traveled.

Let $m$ represent the number of miles.

The total cost is:

$$
\boxed{5+2m}
$$

---

# 15. Example: A Savings Account

You have $100 in a savings account and deposit $15 each week.

Let $w$ represent the number of weeks.

The amount of money in the account is:

$$
\boxed{100+15w}
$$

---

# 16. Example: A Discount

A jacket originally costs $p$ dollars. It is discounted by $20$.

The sale price is:

$$
\boxed{p-20}
$$

If the jacket is discounted by 20%, the expression would be different:

$$
\boxed{0.80p}
$$

because you pay 80% of the original price.

---

# 17. Example: Comparing Two Quantities

A large box contains 3 times as many items as a small box.

Let $s$ represent the number of items in the small box.

The number of items in the large box is:

$$
\boxed{3s}
$$

# 18. Common Mistakes

## Mistake 1: Reversing "Less Than"

"7 less than a number":

Correct:

$$
\boxed{x-7}
$$

Not:

$$
7-x
$$

---

## Mistake 2: Forgetting Parentheses

"3 times the sum of $x$ and 4":

Correct:

$$
\boxed{3(x+4)}
$$

Not:

$$
3x+4
$$

---

## Mistake 3: Confusing "More Than" with Multiplication

"5 more than $x$":

$$
\boxed{x+5}
$$

"5 times $x$":

$$
\boxed{5x}
$$

These expressions have completely different meanings.

---

## Mistake 4: Confusing "Per" with Addition

If something costs $8 per item and you buy $n$ items:

$$
\boxed{8n}
$$

"Per" usually indicates a rate of multiplication.

---

# 19. A Quick Translation Strategy

When you see a word problem, ask:

### What Is Unknown?

Choose a variable.

### What Is Happening to the Unknown?

Is it:

* being increased?
* being decreased?
* multiplied?
* divided?
* combined with another quantity?

### What Operation Represents the Situation?

Then write the expression.

---

# GED Test Tip

The GED may ask you to choose an expression that represents a real-world situation.

Always read carefully:

* **More than** usually means addition.
* **Less than** can reverse the order.
* **Times** means multiplication.
* **Per** often represents a rate.
* **The sum of** means addition.
* **The difference between** means subtraction.
* **The product of** means multiplication.
* **The quotient of** means division.

When a number applies to an entire phrase, use parentheses.

For example:

> Twice the difference between a number and 5

First find the difference:

$$
x-5
$$

Then multiply by 2:

$$
\boxed{2(x-5)}
$$

---

# Summary

To translate a word problem into an algebraic expression:

1. Identify the unknown quantity.
2. Represent it with a variable.
3. Look for keywords that indicate mathematical operations.
4. Pay attention to the order of the words.
5. Use parentheses when an operation applies to an entire expression.
6. Check that the expression accurately represents the situation.

Remember:

$$
\boxed{\text{Words}\longrightarrow\text{Operations}\longrightarrow\text{Algebraic Expression}}
$$
