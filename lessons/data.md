# GED Math Lesson: Mean, Median, and Mode

## Goal

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

- Understand what mean, median, and mode represent.
- Calculate the mean of a data set.
- Find the median of an ordered data set.
- Identify the mode.
- Understand the range of a data set.
- Solve GED-style problems involving measures of center.

---

# 1. What Are Measures of Center?

A **measure of center** describes the typical or central value in a set of data.

The three most common measures of center are:

- **Mean** — the average.
- **Median** — the middle value.
- **Mode** — the most frequent value.

### Memory Trick

- **Mean → Add and divide**
- **Median → Middle**
- **Mode → Most often**

---

# 2. Mean

The **mean** is the average of a set of numbers.

To find the mean:

1. Add all the numbers.
2. Divide the total by the number of values.

The formula is:

$$
\text{Mean}=\frac{\text{Sum of all values}}{\text{Number of values}}
$$

### Example

Find the mean of:

$$
4,\quad 6,\quad 8,\quad 10
$$

First, add the values:

$$
4+6+8+10=28
$$

There are 4 values.

$$
\text{Mean}=\frac{28}{4}
$$

$$
\boxed{7}
$$

Therefore, the mean is 7.

---

# 3. Median

The **median** is the middle value of an ordered data set.

### Steps

1. Put the numbers in order from least to greatest.
2. Find the middle number.

### Example

Find the median:

$$
8,\quad 3,\quad 10,\quad 5,\quad 7
$$

First, order the numbers:

$$
3,\quad 5,\quad \boxed{7},\quad 8,\quad 10
$$

The middle number is 7.

Therefore, the median is 7.

---

# 4. Median with an Even Number of Values

If there is an even number of values, there are two middle numbers.

Find their average.

### Example

Find the median:

$$
2,\quad 4,\quad 7,\quad 10,\quad 12,\quad 15
$$

The two middle values are 7 and 10.

Find their average:

$$
\frac{7+10}{2}
$$

$$
\frac{17}{2}=8.5
$$

Therefore, the median is:

$$
\boxed{8.5}
$$

---

# 5. Mode

The **mode** is the value that appears most often in a data set.

### Example

Find the mode:

$$
2,\quad 4,\quad 4,\quad 5,\quad 7,\quad 4,\quad 9
$$

The number 4 appears three times.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Mode}=4}
$$

---

# 6. Data Sets with More Than One Mode

A data set can have more than one mode.

### Example

Consider:

$$
2,\quad 3,\quad 3,\quad 5,\quad 5,\quad 7
$$

The numbers 3 and 5 both appear twice.

Therefore, the data set has two modes:

$$
\boxed{3\text{ and }5}
$$

This is called **bimodal**.

---

# 7. Data Sets with No Mode

A data set has no mode if every value appears the same number of times.

### Example

$$
1,\quad 3,\quad 5,\quad 7,\quad 9
$$

Every number appears once.

Therefore, there is no mode.

---

# 8. Comparing Mean, Median, and Mode

Consider the data set:

$$
2,\quad 4,\quad 4,\quad 6,\quad 9
$$

### Mean

$$
\frac{2+4+4+6+9}{5}
$$

$$
\frac{25}{5}=5
$$

Mean:

$$
\boxed{5}
$$

### Median

The numbers are already ordered.

The middle value is:

$$
\boxed{4}
$$

### Mode

The number 4 appears most often.

$$
\boxed{\text{Mode}=4}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Mean}=5,\quad\text{Median}=4,\quad\text{Mode}=4}
$$

---

# 9. Range

The **range** describes the spread of a data set.

The formula is:

$$
\text{Range}=\text{Maximum}-\text{Minimum}
$$

### Example

Find the range:

$$
3,\quad 7,\quad 9,\quad 12,\quad 15
$$

The maximum is 15.

The minimum is 3.

Therefore:

$$
\text{Range}=15-3
$$

$$
\boxed{12}
$$

---

# 10. The Effect of an Outlier

An **outlier** is a value that is unusually large or small compared with the other values.

Consider:

$$
4,\quad 5,\quad 5,\quad 6,\quad 20
$$

The number 20 is much larger than the other values.

### Mean

$$
\text{Mean}=\frac{4+5+5+6+20}{5}
$$

$$
\text{Mean}=\frac{40}{5}
$$

$$
\boxed{8}
$$

### Median

The middle value is:

$$
\boxed{5}
$$

The outlier 20 increases the mean, but the median remains 5.

### Important

- **The mean is affected by outliers.**
- **The median is generally less affected by outliers.**

---

# 11. When Should You Use the Mean or Median?

### Use the Mean

The mean is useful when:

- The data is fairly balanced.
- There are no extreme outliers.
- You want the overall average.

### Use the Median

The median is useful when:

- The data contains extreme values.
- The data is not evenly distributed.
- You want the middle value.

### Example

Consider the incomes:

$$
\$30{,}000,\quad \$32{,}000,\quad \$35{,}000,\quad \$38{,}000,\quad \$1{,}000{,}000
$$

The income of $1,000,000 is an outlier.

The **median** would better represent a typical income than the mean.

---

# 12. GED-Style Example: Finding the Mean

A student receives the following scores:

$$
78,\quad 84,\quad 90,\quad 88,\quad 80
$$

What is the mean score?

Add the scores:

$$
78+84+90+88+80=420
$$

There are 5 scores.

$$
\text{Mean}=\frac{420}{5}
$$

$$
\boxed{84}
$$

Therefore, the mean score is 84.

---

# 13. GED-Style Example: Finding the Median

Find the median of:

$$
12,\quad 5,\quad 8,\quad 15,\quad 10,\quad 7
$$

First, order the numbers:

$$
5,\quad 7,\quad 8,\quad 10,\quad 12,\quad 15
$$

There are 6 values, so find the average of the two middle values:

$$
\frac{8+10}{2}
$$

$$
\boxed{9}
$$

Therefore, the median is 9.

---

# 14. GED-Style Example: Finding the Mode and Range

Consider the data set:

$$
6,\quad 8,\quad 8,\quad 10,\quad 12,\quad 15
$$

### Mode

The number 8 appears most often.

$$
\boxed{\text{Mode}=8}
$$

### Range

$$
\text{Range}=15-6
$$

$$
\boxed{9}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Mode}=8,\quad\text{Range}=9}
$$

---

# 15. GED-Style Example: Finding a Missing Value

The mean of five numbers is 12.

Four of the numbers are:

$$
8,\quad 10,\quad 14,\quad 15
$$

Find the missing number.

First, find the total sum needed.

$$
\text{Total}=12\times5
$$

$$
\text{Total}=60
$$

Add the known values:

$$
8+10+14+15=47
$$

Let the missing number be \(x\):

$$
47+x=60
$$

Subtract 47:

$$
x=13
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{13}
$$

# Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Forgetting to Order the Numbers

Always order the numbers before finding the median.

### Mistake 2: Dividing by the Wrong Number

For the mean, divide by the **number of values**, not by the largest value.

### Mistake 3: Confusing Median and Mode

- **Median** = middle value.
- **Mode** = most frequent value.

### Mistake 4: Forgetting to Average Two Middle Values

When there is an even number of data values, average the two middle values.

### Mistake 5: Ignoring Outliers

An extreme value can significantly change the mean.

---

# GED Quick Reference

| Measure | Meaning | How to Find It |
|---|---|---|
| Mean | Average | Add all values and divide |
| Median | Middle value | Order the data and find the middle |
| Mode | Most frequent value | Find the value appearing most often |
| Range | Spread | Maximum − Minimum |

## Memory Trick

- **Mean = Add and divide**
- **Median = Middle**
- **Mode = Most**
- **Range = Biggest − Smallest**

When you see a data set, ask yourself:

> What does the question want me to find?

Then choose the correct measure and follow the appropriate steps.
