# Operations with Rational Expressions

## Learning Goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

* Identify rational expressions.
* Simplify rational expressions.
* Add and subtract rational expressions.
* Multiply rational expressions.
* Divide rational expressions.
* Find common denominators.
* Factor expressions to simplify rational expressions.
* Identify values that make a denominator zero.

---

# 1. What Is a Rational Expression?

A **rational expression** is a fraction that contains polynomials in the numerator, denominator, or both.

Examples:

$$
\frac{x+3}{x-2}
$$

$$
\frac{2x}{5}
$$

$$
\frac{x^2-9}{x+3}
$$

A rational expression is similar to a numerical fraction.

For example:

$$
\frac{6}{8}
$$

can be simplified to:

$$
\frac{3}{4}
$$

Similarly:

$$
\frac{x^2-9}{x+3}
$$

can be simplified by factoring.

---

# 2. The Denominator Cannot Equal Zero

A denominator can never equal zero because division by zero is undefined.

For example:

$$
\frac{5}{x-3}
$$

The denominator is zero when:

$$
x-3=0
$$

Therefore:

$$
x=3
$$

So, $x$ cannot equal 3.

We write:

$$
\boxed{x\ne3}
$$

---

# 3. Simplifying Rational Expressions

To simplify a rational expression:

1. Factor the numerator.
2. Factor the denominator.
3. Cancel common factors.

---

## Example

Simplify:

$$
\frac{x^2-9}{x+3}
$$

Factor the numerator:

$$
x^2-9=(x-3)(x+3)
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac{(x-3)(x+3)}{x+3}
$$

Cancel the common factor:

$$
\boxed{x-3}
$$

Remember:

$$
x\ne-3
$$

because the original denominator becomes zero when $x=-3$.

---

# 4. Adding Rational Expressions with the Same Denominator

When rational expressions have the same denominator, add the numerators and keep the denominator.

$$
\frac{a}{c}+\frac{b}{c}
=
\frac{a+b}{c}
$$

---

## Example

Add:

$$
\frac{3}{x}+\frac{5}{x}
$$

Add the numerators:

$$
\boxed{\frac{8}{x}}
$$

---

## Example

Add:

$$
\frac{x+2}{x-1}+\frac{3x}{x-1}
$$

Since the denominators are the same:

$$
\frac{x+2+3x}{x-1}
$$

Combine like terms:

$$
\boxed{\frac{4x+2}{x-1}}
$$

---

# 5. Subtracting Rational Expressions with the Same Denominator

Subtract the numerators and keep the denominator.

$$
\frac{a}{c}-\frac{b}{c}
=
\frac{a-b}{c}
$$

---

## Example

Subtract:

$$
\frac{7}{x}-\frac{2}{x}
$$

$$
\boxed{\frac{5}{x}}
$$

---

## Example

Subtract:

$$
\frac{5x+4}{x+2}-\frac{2x-1}{x+2}
$$

Subtract the entire second numerator:

$$
\frac{(5x+4)-(2x-1)}{x+2}
$$

Distribute the negative sign:

$$
\frac{5x+4-2x+1}{x+2}
$$

Combine like terms:

$$
\boxed{\frac{3x+5}{x+2}}
$$

---

# 6. Adding Rational Expressions with Different Denominators

When denominators are different:

1. Find the least common denominator (LCD).
2. Rewrite each fraction using the LCD.
3. Add the numerators.
4. Simplify.

---

## Example

Add:

$$
\frac{1}{x}+\frac{1}{2}
$$

The LCD is:

$$
2x
$$

Rewrite each fraction:

$$
\frac{1}{x}=\frac{2}{2x}
$$

$$
\frac{1}{2}=\frac{x}{2x}
$$

Now add:

$$
\frac{2}{2x}+\frac{x}{2x}
$$

$$
\boxed{\frac{x+2}{2x}}
$$

---

# 7. Example with Polynomial Denominators

Add:

$$
\frac{2}{x+1}+\frac{3}{x-1}
$$

The LCD is:

$$
(x+1)(x-1)
$$

Rewrite the fractions:

$$
\frac{2(x-1)}{(x+1)(x-1)}
+
\frac{3(x+1)}{(x-1)(x+1)}
$$

Expand the numerators:

$$
\frac{2x-2+3x+3}{(x+1)(x-1)}
$$

Combine like terms:

$$
\boxed{\frac{5x+1}{(x+1)(x-1)}}
$$

---

# 8. Multiplying Rational Expressions

To multiply rational expressions:

1. Factor all numerators and denominators.
2. Cancel common factors.
3. Multiply the remaining factors.

The rule is:

$$
\frac{a}{b}\cdot\frac{c}{d}
=
\frac{ac}{bd}
$$

---

## Example

Multiply:

$$
\frac{3}{x}\cdot\frac{x}{5}
$$

Cancel $x$:

$$
\frac{3}{5}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\frac{3}{5}}
$$

---

# 9. Multiplying with Factoring

Multiply:

$$
\frac{x^2-9}{x+2}\cdot\frac{x+2}{x+3}
$$

Factor:

$$
x^2-9=(x-3)(x+3)
$$

Substitute:

$$
\frac{(x-3)(x+3)}{x+2}
\cdot
\frac{x+2}{x+3}
$$

Cancel common factors:

$$
\boxed{x-3}
$$

---

# 10. Dividing Rational Expressions

To divide rational expressions:

1. Keep the first fraction.
2. Change division to multiplication.
3. Flip the second fraction.
4. Factor and simplify.

This is called **Keep, Change, Flip**.

$$
\frac{a}{b}\div\frac{c}{d}
=
\frac{a}{b}\cdot\frac{d}{c}
$$

---

## Example

Divide:

$$
\frac{3}{x}\div\frac{5}{2}
$$

Keep the first fraction:

$$
\frac{3}{x}
$$

Change division to multiplication:

$$
\frac{3}{x}\cdot
$$

Flip the second fraction:

$$
\frac{2}{5}
$$

Now multiply:

$$
\boxed{\frac{6}{5x}}
$$

---

# 11. Dividing Polynomial Rational Expressions

Divide:

$$
\frac{x^2-9}{x+2}
\div
\frac{x-3}{x+2}
$$

### Step 1: Change division to multiplication

$$
\frac{x^2-9}{x+2}
\cdot
\frac{x+2}{x-3}
$$

### Step 2: Factor

$$
x^2-9=(x-3)(x+3)
$$

So:

$$
\frac{(x-3)(x+3)}{x+2}
\cdot
\frac{x+2}{x-3}
$$

### Step 3: Cancel common factors

$$
\boxed{x+3}
$$

---

# 12. Adding and Subtracting: The LCD

The **least common denominator (LCD)** is the smallest expression that contains all the factors needed in the denominators.

For example:

$$
\frac{1}{x}
\quad \text{and} \quad
\frac{1}{x+2}
$$

The LCD is:

$$
\boxed{x(x+2)}
$$

---

# 13. Example: Finding the LCD

Find the LCD of:

$$
\frac{1}{x^2}
\quad \text{and} \quad
\frac{1}{3x}
$$

The denominator factors are:

$$
x^2
$$

and:

$$
3x
$$

The LCD is:

$$
\boxed{3x^2}
$$

---

# 14. A Complete Addition Example

Simplify:

$$
\frac{2}{x}+\frac{3}{x+1}
$$

### Step 1: Find the LCD

$$
x(x+1)
$$

### Step 2: Rewrite each fraction

$$
\frac{2(x+1)}{x(x+1)}
+
\frac{3x}{x(x+1)}
$$

### Step 3: Expand the numerator

$$
\frac{2x+2+3x}{x(x+1)}
$$

### Step 4: Combine like terms

$$
\boxed{\frac{5x+2}{x(x+1)}}
$$

---

# 15. A Complete Subtraction Example

Simplify:

$$
\frac{3}{x-2}-\frac{1}{x+2}
$$

The LCD is:

$$
(x-2)(x+2)
$$

Rewrite:

$$
\frac{3(x+2)}{(x-2)(x+2)}
-
\frac{x-2}{(x+2)(x-2)}
$$

Expand:

$$
\frac{3x+6-x+2}{(x-2)(x+2)}
$$

Combine:

$$
\boxed{\frac{2x+8}{(x-2)(x+2)}}
$$

---

# 16. Common Mistakes

## Mistake 1: Adding Denominators

Incorrect:

$$
\frac{2}{x}+\frac{3}{x}
=
\frac{5}{2x}
$$

Correct:

$$
\boxed{\frac{5}{x}}
$$

You add the numerators, not the denominators.

---

## Mistake 2: Canceling Terms Instead of Factors

You cannot cancel terms across addition.

Incorrect:

$$
\frac{x+3}{x}
$$

You cannot cancel the $x$ because the numerator is a sum.

However:

$$
\frac{x(x+3)}{x}
$$

can be simplified:

$$
\boxed{x+3}
$$

because $x$ is a factor.

---

## Mistake 3: Forgetting to Flip When Dividing

Incorrect:

$$
\frac{a}{b}\div\frac{c}{d}
=
\frac{a}{b}\cdot\frac{c}{d}
$$

Correct:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{a}{b}\div\frac{c}{d}
=
\frac{a}{b}\cdot\frac{d}{c}
}
$$

---

## Mistake 4: Forgetting to Factor

Before multiplying or simplifying, always check whether the polynomials can be factored.

For example:

$$
x^2-16
$$

can be factored:

$$
(x-4)(x+4)
$$

Factoring often reveals common factors that can be canceled.

---

# 17. A Reliable Strategy

### For Addition and Subtraction

1. Find the LCD.
2. Rewrite each fraction.
3. Combine the numerators.
4. Simplify.

### For Multiplication

1. Factor.
2. Cancel common factors.
3. Multiply.

### For Division

1. Keep the first fraction.
2. Change division to multiplication.
3. Flip the second fraction.
4. Factor and simplify.

---

# GED Test Tip

> **Adding and subtracting require a common denominator.**

But:

> **Multiplying and dividing do not require a common denominator.**

For multiplication:

$$
\frac{a}{b}\cdot\frac{c}{d}
$$

multiply straight across.

For division:

$$
\frac{a}{b}\div\frac{c}{d}
$$

flip the second fraction and multiply.

---

# Summary

To work with rational expressions:

### Add

Find a common denominator, then add the numerators.

### Subtract

Find a common denominator, then subtract the numerators carefully.

### Multiply

Factor, cancel common factors, and multiply.

### Divide

Keep, change, flip, and multiply.

Always remember:

$$
\boxed{\text{The denominator can never equal zero.}}
$$

and:

$$
\boxed{\text{You can cancel factors, not terms.}}
$$
