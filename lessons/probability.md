# GED Math Lesson: Probability of an Event

## Goal

Learn how to find the probability of an event and solve GED problems involving:

* favorable outcomes
* possible outcomes
* coins
* dice
* cards
* spinners
* games
* real-world events

---

# 1. What Is Probability?

**Probability** measures how likely an event is to happen.

The probability of an event is always between:

$$
0
$$

and:

$$
1
$$

It can also be written as a fraction, decimal, or percent.

---

## Probability Scale

$$
\boxed{0\leq P(E)\leq1}
$$

where $P(E)$ means the probability of event $E$.

### Probability of $0$

The event is impossible.

$$
\boxed{P(E)=0}
$$

### Probability of $1$

The event is certain.

$$
\boxed{P(E)=1}
$$

---

# 2. The Basic Probability Formula

The probability of an event is:

$$
\boxed{
P(E)=
\frac{\text{Number of Favorable Outcomes}}
{\text{Total Number of Possible Outcomes}}
}
$$

---

## Example

A bag contains:

* 3 red marbles
* 2 blue marbles

There are:

$$
3+2=5
$$

total marbles.

The probability of selecting a red marble is:

$$
P(\text{red})=\frac{3}{5}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\frac{3}{5}}
$$

or:

$$
\boxed{0.6}
$$

or:

$$
\boxed{60\%}
$$

---

# 3. Favorable Outcomes and Possible Outcomes

The **favorable outcomes** are the outcomes that match the event you are looking for.

The **total possible outcomes** include every possible result.

### Example

A standard six-sided die has:

$$
6
$$

possible outcomes:

$$
1,2,3,4,5,6
$$

What is the probability of rolling a 4?

There is:

$$
1
$$

favorable outcome.

Therefore:

$$
P(4)=\frac{1}{6}
$$

---

# 4. Probability of Rolling an Even Number

A standard die has the numbers:

$$
1,2,3,4,5,6
$$

The even numbers are:

$$
2,4,6
$$

There are:

$$
3
$$

favorable outcomes out of:

$$
6
$$

total outcomes.

$$
P(\text{even})=\frac{3}{6}
$$

Simplify:

$$
\boxed{\frac{1}{2}}
$$

Therefore, the probability is:

$$
\boxed{\frac{1}{2}=0.5=50\%}
$$

---

# 5. Probability of a Coin Toss

A fair coin has two possible outcomes:

$$
\text{Heads and Tails}
$$

The probability of getting heads is:

$$
P(\text{Heads})=\frac{1}{2}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\frac{1}{2}=50\%}
$$

The probability of getting tails is also:

$$
\boxed{\frac{1}{2}}
$$

---

# 6. Probability of Drawing an Object

### Example

A box contains:

* 4 green balls
* 3 yellow balls
* 5 red balls

The total number of balls is:

$$
4+3+5=12
$$

What is the probability of selecting a yellow ball?

There are:

$$
3
$$

yellow balls.

Therefore:

$$
P(\text{yellow})=\frac{3}{12}
$$

Simplify:

$$
\boxed{\frac{1}{4}}
$$

---

# 7. Probability as a Decimal or Percent

A probability can be written in different forms.

For example:

$$
\frac{1}{4}
$$

Convert to a decimal:

$$
1\div4=0.25
$$

Convert to a percent:

$$
0.25\times100=25\%
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\frac{1}{4}=0.25=25\%}
$$

---

# 8. The Complement of an Event

The **complement** of an event is the event that does **not** happen.

The formula is:

$$
\boxed{P(\text{not }E)=1-P(E)}
$$

---

## Example

The probability of rain tomorrow is:

$$
0.3
$$

The probability that it does not rain is:

$$
1-0.3=0.7
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{P(\text{no rain})=0.7}
$$

or:

$$
\boxed{70\%}
$$

---

# 9. Probability of "Not"

### Example

A bag contains 10 marbles.

The probability of selecting a blue marble is:

$$
\frac{3}{10}
$$

The probability of not selecting a blue marble is:

$$
1-\frac{3}{10}
$$

$$
=\frac{7}{10}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\frac{7}{10}}
$$

---

# 10. Probability of Two Independent Events

When two independent events both happen, multiply their probabilities.

### Example

A fair coin is tossed twice.

What is the probability of getting heads both times?

The probability of heads on the first toss is:

$$
\frac{1}{2}
$$

The probability of heads on the second toss is:

$$
\frac{1}{2}
$$

Multiply:

$$
\frac{1}{2}\times\frac{1}{2}
=
\frac{1}{4}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\frac{1}{4}}
$$

---

# 11. Probability of "Or"

When events cannot happen at the same time, add their probabilities.

### Example

What is the probability of rolling a 1 or a 6 on a fair six-sided die?

The probability of rolling a 1 is:

$$
\frac{1}{6}
$$

The probability of rolling a 6 is:

$$
\frac{1}{6}
$$

Therefore:

$$
P(1\text{ or }6)
=
\frac{1}{6}+\frac{1}{6}
$$

$$
=\frac{2}{6}
$$

$$
\boxed{\frac{1}{3}}
$$

---

# 12. Probability Without Replacement

Sometimes an object is not returned after being selected.

### Example

A bag contains:

* 3 red balls
* 2 blue balls

A red ball is selected and not replaced.

What is the probability of selecting another red ball?

Initially, there are:

$$
5
$$

balls.

After selecting one red ball:

* Red balls remaining: $2$
* Total balls remaining: $4$

Therefore:

$$
P(\text{second red})
=
\frac{2}{4}
$$

$$
\boxed{\frac{1}{2}}
$$

---

# 13. Experimental Probability

**Experimental probability** is based on actual results from an experiment.

### Formula

$$
\boxed{
\text{Experimental Probability}
=
\frac{\text{Number of Times Event Occurred}}
{\text{Total Number of Trials}}
}
$$

---

## Example

A coin is tossed 50 times.

It lands on heads 28 times.

The experimental probability of heads is:

$$
\frac{28}{50}
$$

$$
=0.56
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{56\%}
$$

---

# 14. Theoretical Probability

**Theoretical probability** is based on what should happen if all outcomes are equally likely.

### Example

For a fair six-sided die:

$$
P(\text{rolling a 6})
=
\frac{1}{6}
$$

This is the theoretical probability.

---

# 15. GED-Style Example

A spinner has 8 equal sections:

* 3 red
* 2 blue
* 3 green

What is the probability of landing on blue?

There are:

$$
2
$$

favorable outcomes.

There are:

$$
8
$$

total outcomes.

Therefore:

$$
P(\text{blue})=\frac{2}{8}
$$

Simplify:

$$
\boxed{\frac{1}{4}}
$$

or:

$$
\boxed{25\%}
$$

---

# How to Solve Probability Problems

1. Identify the event.
2. Count the favorable outcomes.
3. Count all possible outcomes.
4. Use:

$$
\boxed{
P(E)=
\frac{\text{Favorable Outcomes}}
{\text{Total Outcomes}}
}
$$

5. Simplify the fraction if possible.
6. Convert to a decimal or percent if required.

---

# Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Using the Wrong Denominator

The denominator must represent:

$$
\boxed{\text{All possible outcomes}}
$$

---

### Mistake 2: Forgetting to Count All Favorable Outcomes

If you want an even number on a die, the favorable outcomes are:

$$
2,4,6
$$

not just one number.

---

### Mistake 3: Confusing Probability with the Number of Outcomes

If a bag contains 3 red balls out of 10 total balls:

$$
3
$$

is the number of favorable outcomes.

The probability is:

$$
\frac{3}{10}
$$

---

### Mistake 4: Forgetting to Simplify

For example:

$$
\frac{4}{8}
$$

can be simplified to:

$$
\boxed{\frac{1}{2}}
$$

---

# GED Tips

* Probability is always between $0$ and $1$.
* Use:

$$
\boxed{
P(E)=
\frac{\text{Favorable Outcomes}}
{\text{Total Possible Outcomes}}
}
$$

* $0$ means impossible.
* $1$ means certain.
* For independent events happening together, multiply probabilities.
* For mutually exclusive events, add probabilities.
* The complement of an event is:

$$
\boxed{1-P(E)}
$$

---

## Memory Trick

### Probability asks:

> How many ways can the event happen?

over:

> How many total outcomes are possible?

$$
\boxed{
\text{Probability}
=
\frac{\text{What I want}}
{\text{Everything possible}}
}
$$
