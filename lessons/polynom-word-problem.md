# Writing Polynomials from Problem Situations

## Learning Goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

* Identify unknown quantities in real-world situations.
* Use variables to represent unknown values.
* Translate words and phrases into algebraic expressions.
* Create polynomials to represent real-world situations.
* Use polynomial expressions to represent area, cost, distance, and other quantities.
* Recognize when a situation requires combining multiple terms.

---

# 1. What Is a Polynomial?

A **polynomial** is an algebraic expression made up of terms.

Examples:

$$
3x+5
$$

$$
x^2+4x+3
$$

$$
2x^3-5x^2+x-7
$$

A polynomial can contain:

* Numbers
* Variables
* Addition
* Subtraction
* Multiplication
* Nonnegative whole-number exponents

---

# 2. From Words to Algebra

Many real-world situations can be represented using polynomials.

For example:

> A number is increased by 5.

Let the number be $x$.

The polynomial is:

$$
\boxed{x+5}
$$

Another example:

> Three times the square of a number, plus 4.

The square of a number is:

$$
x^2
$$

Three times the square is:

$$
3x^2
$$

Adding 4 gives:

$$
\boxed{3x^2+4}
$$

---

# 3. Choosing a Variable

The first step is to identify the unknown quantity.

### Example

> A farmer has an unknown number of chickens.

Let:

$$
c=\text{number of chickens}
$$

If the farmer buys 10 more chickens:

$$
\boxed{c+10}
$$

If the farmer has 3 times as many chickens as another farmer who has $x$ chickens:

$$
\boxed{3x}
$$

# 4. Common Word-to-Symbol Translations

| Words                         | Algebraic Expression |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------- |
| a number                      | $x$                  |
| a number squared              | $x^2$                |
| a number cubed                | $x^3$                |
| 5 more than a number          | $x+5$                |
| 5 less than a number          | $x-5$                |
| twice a number                | $2x$                 |
| three times a number          | $3x$                 |
| the product of $x$ and $y$    | $xy$                 |
| the sum of $x$ and $y$        | $x+y$                |
| the difference of $x$ and $y$ | $x-y$                |
| the square of a number plus 4 | $x^2+4$              |
| twice the sum of $x$ and 3    | $2(x+3)$             |

# 5. Writing a Polynomial from a Situation

When creating a polynomial:

### Step 1: Identify the unknown quantity.

Choose a variable.

### Step 2: Identify the relationships.

Look for words such as:

* more
* less
* times
* squared
* cubed
* product
* sum
* difference

### Step 3: Translate each part.

Convert words into mathematical symbols.

### Step 4: Combine the terms.

Write the complete polynomial.

---

# 6. Example: A Number Situation

Write a polynomial for:

> The square of a number increased by three times the number and then increased by 5.

Let the number be:

$$
x
$$

The square of the number:

$$
x^2
$$

Three times the number:

$$
3x
$$

Add 5:

$$
\boxed{x^2+3x+5}
$$

---

# 7. Example: A Cost Situation

A company charges a fixed fee of $50 plus $10 for each item purchased.

Let:

$$
n=\text{number of items}
$$

The cost of the items is:

$$
10n
$$

The total cost is:

$$
\boxed{10n+50}
$$

This is a linear polynomial.

---

# 8. Example: A Cost with a Quadratic Term

A company charges a fixed fee of $20, $5 for each item, and an additional cost of $2 for the square of the number of items.

Let:

$$
n=\text{number of items}
$$

The total cost is:

$$
\boxed{2n^2+5n+20}
$$

This is a quadratic polynomial.

---

# 9. Using Area to Create Polynomials

Geometry is a common way to create polynomial expressions.

The area of a rectangle is:

$$
A=lw
$$

Suppose a rectangle has:

* Length: $x+5$
* Width: $x+2$

Then:

$$
A=(x+5)(x+2)
$$

Expand:

$$
A=x^2+2x+5x+10
$$

Combine like terms:

$$
\boxed{A=x^2+7x+10}
$$

---

# 10. Example: A Square

A square has a side length of:

$$
x+3
$$

The area of a square is:

$$
A=s^2
$$

Therefore:

$$
A=(x+3)^2
$$

Expand:

$$
A=(x+3)(x+3)
$$

$$
A=x^2+3x+3x+9
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{A=x^2+6x+9}
$$

---

# 11. Example: Perimeter

A rectangle has a length of $x+4$ and a width of $x+2$.

The perimeter is:

$$
P=2l+2w
$$

Substitute:

$$
P=2(x+4)+2(x+2)
$$

Expand:

$$
P=2x+8+2x+4
$$

Combine like terms:

$$
\boxed{P=4x+12}
$$

---

# 12. Representing Distance

Suppose a person travels:

* $x^2$ miles in the morning.
* $3x+5$ miles in the afternoon.

The total distance is:

$$
x^2+(3x+5)
$$

Simplify:

$$
\boxed{x^2+3x+5}
$$

---

# 13. Representing Revenue

A business sells $x$ products.

The price per product is:

$$
x+5
$$

Revenue is:

$$
\text{number of products}\times\text{price per product}
$$

Therefore:

$$
R=x(x+5)
$$

Expand:

$$
\boxed{R=x^2+5x}
$$

---

# 14. Representing Profit

Suppose:

* Revenue is $x^2+10x$.
* Cost is $4x+20$.

Profit is:

$$
\text{Revenue}-\text{Cost}
$$

Therefore:

$$
P=(x^2+10x)-(4x+20)
$$

Simplify:

$$
P=x^2+10x-4x-20
$$

$$
\boxed{P=x^2+6x-20}
$$

---

# 15. Translating Products into Polynomials

Sometimes a situation involves multiplication.

For example:

> The length of a rectangle is 3 more than a number, and the width is 2 more than the number.

Let the number be:

$$
x
$$

Length:

$$
x+3
$$

Width:

$$
x+2
$$

Area:

$$
A=(x+3)(x+2)
$$

Expand:

$$
A=x^2+5x+6
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{A=x^2+5x+6}
$$

---

# 16. Using Multiple Variables

Some situations involve more than one unknown.

Suppose a rectangle has:

* Length $l$
* Width $w$

Its area is:

$$
\boxed{lw}
$$

If the length is 3 more than the width:

$$
l=w+3
$$

Then the area can be written as:

$$
A=w(w+3)
$$

Expanding:

$$
\boxed{A=w^2+3w}
$$

---

# 17. Word Order and Parentheses

Pay close attention to whether a phrase applies to one term or an entire expression.

### Example

> Twice the sum of a number and 4

First find the sum:

$$
x+4
$$

Then multiply the entire sum by 2:

$$
\boxed{2(x+4)}
$$

This is different from:

$$
2x+4
$$

---

# 18. Example: A Number Pattern

The first number is $x$.

The second number is 2 more than $x$:

$$
x+2
$$

The third number is 4 more than $x$:

$$
x+4
$$

The sum of the three numbers is:

$$
x+(x+2)+(x+4)
$$

Simplify:

$$
3x+6
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{3x+6}
$$

---

# 19. Example: Consecutive Integers

Let the first integer be:

$$
x
$$

The next consecutive integer is:

$$
x+1
$$

The third consecutive integer is:

$$
x+2
$$

Their product is:

$$
\boxed{x(x+1)(x+2)}
$$

This expression represents the product of three consecutive integers.

# 20. A Complete Word-to-Polynomial Example

### Problem

A rectangular garden has a length that is 5 meters greater than its width. Let the width be $x$ meters. Write a polynomial representing the area.

### Step 1: Identify the width

$$
w=x
$$

### Step 2: Write the length

The length is 5 greater than the width:

$$
l=x+5
$$

### Step 3: Use the area formula

$$
A=lw
$$

Substitute:

$$
A=x(x+5)
$$

### Step 4: Expand

$$
\boxed{A=x^2+5x}
$$

# 21. Common Mistakes

## Mistake 1: Forgetting Multiplication

If a rectangle has length $x+3$ and width $x+2$, its area is:

$$
(x+3)(x+2)
$$

not:

$$
x+3+x+2
$$

Area requires multiplication.

---

## Mistake 2: Misinterpreting "Twice the Sum"

"Twice the sum of $x$ and 5":

$$
\boxed{2(x+5)}
$$

Not:

$$
2x+5
$$

---

## Mistake 3: Confusing "Square of a Number"

"The square of a number plus 4":

$$
\boxed{x^2+4}
$$

"The square of the sum of a number and 4":

$$
\boxed{(x+4)^2}
$$

These expressions are different.

---

## Mistake 4: Forgetting to Expand

If the question asks for a polynomial in standard form, you may need to expand:

$$
(x+3)(x+2)
$$

into:

$$
\boxed{x^2+5x+6}
$$

---

# 22. A Reliable Strategy

When creating a polynomial from a problem situation:

### Ask yourself:

1. What quantity is unknown?
2. What variable represents it?
3. What operations are described?
4. Does a phrase apply to one term or the entire expression?
5. Does the final answer need to be expanded?

Then translate the situation step by step.

# GED Test Tip

The GED may give you a real-world situation and ask you to choose the polynomial that represents it.

Pay special attention to:

* **Square**: $x^2$
* **Cube**: $x^3$
* **Product**: multiplication
* **Sum**: addition
* **Difference**: subtraction
* **Twice**: $2x$
* **Three times**: $3x$
* **Area**: length $\times$ width
* **Perimeter**: add all side lengths
* **Revenue**: quantity $\times$ price
* **Profit**: revenue $-$ cost

# Summary

To create a polynomial from a problem situation:

1. Identify the unknown quantity.
2. Choose a variable.
3. Translate words into mathematical operations.
4. Use parentheses when necessary.
5. Combine or expand terms when needed.
6. Check that the polynomial represents the situation correctly.

Remember:

$$
\boxed{\text{Real Situation} \longrightarrow \text{Variables} \longrightarrow \text{Operations} \longrightarrow \text{Polynomial}}
$$

For example:

> A rectangle has a length of $x+5$ and a width of $x+2$.

Area:

$$
A=(x+5)(x+2)
$$

Expanded polynomial:

$$
\boxed{A=x^2+7x+10}
$$
