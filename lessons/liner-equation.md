# Linear Equations

## Learning Goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

* Understand what a linear equation is.
* Identify the parts of a linear equation.
* Solve one-step equations.
* Solve two-step equations.
* Solve multi-step equations.
* Solve equations with variables on both sides.
* Solve equations with parentheses.
* Solve equations containing fractions and decimals.
* Identify equations with no solution or infinitely many solutions.
* Check whether a solution is correct.
* Write linear equations from word problems.
* Solve real-world problems using linear equations.

---

# 1. What Is an Equation?

An **equation** is a mathematical statement that says two expressions are equal.

An equation contains an equal sign:

$$
=
$$

For example:

$$
3x+5=20
$$

The expression on the left side is:

$$
3x+5
$$

The expression on the right side is:

$$
20
$$

The goal is to find the value of the variable that makes both sides equal.

---

# 2. What Is a Linear Equation?

A **linear equation** is an equation in which the variable has an exponent of 1.

Examples:

$$
x+5=12
$$

$$
3x-7=14
$$

$$
2x+4=5x-8
$$

A linear equation may contain:

* One variable
* Numbers
* Addition
* Subtraction
* Multiplication
* Division

---

# 3. Equations That Are Not Linear

These are not linear equations:

$$
x^2+3=12
$$

because the variable has an exponent of 2.

Also:

$$
\sqrt{x}=5
$$

because the variable is inside a square root.

And:

$$
\frac{1}{x}=4
$$

because the variable is in the denominator.

---

# 4. The Goal of Solving an Equation

The goal is to isolate the variable.

For example:

$$
x+5=12
$$

We want:

$$
x=\text{number}
$$

Subtract 5 from both sides:

$$
x=7
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{x=7}
$$

---

# 5. The Balance Principle

An equation is like a balanced scale.

If you perform an operation on one side, you must perform the same operation on the other side.

For example:

$$
x+4=10
$$

Subtract 4 from both sides:

$$
x+4-4=10-4
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{x=6}
$$

The equation remains balanced because the same operation was performed on both sides.

---

# 6. Inverse Operations

Inverse operations undo each other.

| Operation      | Inverse        |
| -------------- | -------------- |
| Addition       | Subtraction    |
| Subtraction    | Addition       |
| Multiplication | Division       |
| Division       | Multiplication |

Examples:

$$
x+5=12
$$

Subtract 5.

$$
x-5=12
$$

Add 5.

$$
3x=15
$$

Divide by 3.

$$
\frac{x}{4}=6
$$

Multiply by 4.

---

# 7. Solving One-Step Equations

A one-step equation requires one operation to isolate the variable.

---

## Addition

Solve:

$$
x+7=15
$$

Subtract 7 from both sides:

$$
x+7-7=15-7
$$

$$
\boxed{x=8}
$$

---

## Subtraction

Solve:

$$
x-9=4
$$

Add 9 to both sides:

$$
x-9+9=4+9
$$

$$
\boxed{x=13}
$$

---

## Multiplication

Solve:

$$
5x=35
$$

Divide both sides by 5:

$$
\frac{5x}{5}=\frac{35}{5}
$$

$$
\boxed{x=7}
$$

---

## Division

Solve:

$$
\frac{x}{3}=8
$$

Multiply both sides by 3:

$$
\boxed{x=24}
$$

---

# 8. Solving Two-Step Equations

A two-step equation requires two operations.

Example:

$$
3x+5=20
$$

### Step 1: Undo addition

Subtract 5:

$$
3x=15
$$

### Step 2: Undo multiplication

Divide by 3:

$$
\boxed{x=5}
$$

---

## Another Example

Solve:

$$
4x-7=21
$$

Add 7:

$$
4x=28
$$

Divide by 4:

$$
\boxed{x=7}
$$

---

# 9. Solving Equations with Negative Coefficients

Solve:

$$
-3x=18
$$

Divide both sides by $-3$:

$$
x=\frac{18}{-3}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{x=-6}
$$

Always pay attention to the signs.

---

# 10. Solving Multi-Step Equations

A multi-step equation may contain several operations.

Example:

$$
3x+5-2x=18
$$

### Step 1: Combine like terms

$$
3x-2x=x
$$

So:

$$
x+5=18
$$

### Step 2: Subtract 5

$$
\boxed{x=13}
$$

---

# 11. Solving Equations with Parentheses

Use the distributive property first.

Solve:

$$
3(x+4)=21
$$

### Step 1: Distribute

$$
3x+12=21
$$

### Step 2: Subtract 12

$$
3x=9
$$

### Step 3: Divide by 3

$$
\boxed{x=3}
$$

---

## Example

Solve:

$$
2(x-5)+3=15
$$

Distribute:

$$
2x-10+3=15
$$

Combine constants:

$$
2x-7=15
$$

Add 7:

$$
2x=22
$$

Divide by 2:

$$
\boxed{x=11}
$$

---

# 12. Solving Equations with Variables on Both Sides

Example:

$$
5x+3=2x+15
$$

### Step 1: Move variable terms to one side

Subtract $2x$ from both sides:

$$
3x+3=15
$$

### Step 2: Move constants

Subtract 3:

$$
3x=12
$$

### Step 3: Divide

$$
\boxed{x=4}
$$

---

# 13. Choosing Which Variable Side to Use

Consider:

$$
7x+2=3x+18
$$

You can subtract $3x$ from both sides:

$$
4x+2=18
$$

Or subtract $7x$ from both sides:

$$
2=18-4x
$$

Both methods work.

Usually, move the variable term with the smaller coefficient to avoid negative coefficients.

---

# 14. Equations with Fractions

Solve:

$$
\frac{x}{4}+3=8
$$

### Step 1: Subtract 3

$$
\frac{x}{4}=5
$$

### Step 2: Multiply by 4

$$
\boxed{x=20}
$$

---

## Example with Multiple Fractions

Solve:

$$
\frac{x}{2}+\frac{x}{3}=10
$$

The least common denominator is:

$$
6
$$

Multiply every term by 6:

$$
6\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)+6\left(\frac{x}{3}\right)=6(10)
$$

Simplify:

$$
3x+2x=60
$$

$$
5x=60
$$

$$
\boxed{x=12}
$$

---

# 15. Clearing Fractions

Consider:

$$
\frac{x+1}{3}=5
$$

Multiply both sides by 3:

$$
x+1=15
$$

Subtract 1:

$$
\boxed{x=14}
$$

---

# 16. Equations with Decimals

Solve:

$$
0.5x+2=6
$$

Subtract 2:

$$
0.5x=4
$$

Divide by $0.5$:

$$
\boxed{x=8}
$$

---

## Converting Decimals to Fractions

Sometimes it is easier to remove decimals.

For example:

$$
0.25x+1=5
$$

Multiply the entire equation by 100:

$$
25x+100=500
$$

Subtract 100:

$$
25x=400
$$

Divide by 25:

$$
\boxed{x=16}
$$

---

# 17. Equations with Negative Signs

Solve:

$$
-(x+3)=10
$$

Distribute $-1$:

$$
-x-3=10
$$

Add 3:

$$
-x=13
$$

Multiply by $-1$:

$$
\boxed{x=-13}
$$

---

# 18. Checking Your Solution

Always check your answer by substituting it into the original equation.

Suppose:

$$
3x+5=20
$$

and we found:

$$
x=5
$$

Substitute:

$$
3(5)+5=20
$$

$$
15+5=20
$$

$$
20=20
$$

The solution is correct.

---

# 19. No Solution

Sometimes an equation has no possible solution.

Example:

$$
3x+5=3x+10
$$

Subtract $3x$ from both sides:

$$
5=10
$$

This is false.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{No solution}}
$$

---

# 20. Infinitely Many Solutions

Sometimes both sides simplify to the same expression.

Example:

$$
2(x+3)=2x+6
$$

Distribute:

$$
2x+6=2x+6
$$

Both sides are identical.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Infinitely many solutions}}
$$

Every value of $x$ works.

---

# 21. Three Possible Results

When solving a linear equation, you may get:

### One solution

Example:

$$
x=5
$$

### No solution

Example:

$$
5=8
$$

### Infinitely many solutions

Example:

$$
5=5
$$

# 22. Writing Linear Equations from Word Problems

Many GED questions ask you to create an equation from a real-world situation.

### Example

A gym charges a $20 registration fee and $15 per month. After some months, the total cost is $95.

Let:

$$
m=\text{number of months}
$$

The equation is:

$$
20+15m=95
$$

Solve:

$$
15m=75
$$

$$
\boxed{m=5}
$$

The membership lasted 5 months.

# 24. Example: Age Problem

A mother is 24 years older than her daughter. Their ages add up to 60.

Let the daughter's age be:

$$
x
$$

The mother's age is:

$$
x+24
$$

Their total age is:

$$
x+(x+24)=60
$$

Simplify:

$$
2x+24=60
$$

Subtract 24:

$$
2x=36
$$

Divide by 2:

$$
\boxed{x=18}
$$

The daughter is 18 years old.

---

# 25. Example: Consecutive Integers

The sum of two consecutive integers is 41.

Let the first integer be:

$$
x
$$

The next integer is:

$$
x+1
$$

Write the equation:

$$
x+(x+1)=41
$$

Simplify:

$$
2x+1=41
$$

Subtract 1:

$$
2x=40
$$

Divide by 2:

$$
\boxed{x=20}
$$

The two integers are:

$$
\boxed{20\text{ and }21}
$$

---

# 26. Example: Distance and Rate

The formula for distance is:

$$
d=rt
$$

where:

* $d$ = distance
* $r$ = rate
* $t$ = time

If a car travels 60 miles per hour for $t$ hours and travels 180 miles:

$$
180=60t
$$

Divide by 60:

$$
\boxed{t=3}
$$

The trip takes 3 hours.

---

# 27. Example: Perimeter

A rectangle has a length that is 3 units greater than its width. Its perimeter is 30 units.

Let the width be:

$$
x
$$

The length is:

$$
x+3
$$

The perimeter formula is:

$$
P=2l+2w
$$

Substitute:

$$
30=2(x+3)+2x
$$

Distribute:

$$
30=2x+6+2x
$$

Combine:

$$
30=4x+6
$$

Subtract 6:

$$
24=4x
$$

Divide by 4:

$$
\boxed{x=6}
$$

The width is 6 units.

The length is:

$$
6+3=\boxed{9}
$$

---

# 28. Example: Percent Problems

A shirt originally costs $80. It is discounted by 25%.

The discount is:

$$
0.25(80)=20
$$

The sale price is:

$$
80-20=60
$$

Alternatively, the sale price is 75% of the original price:

$$
0.75(80)=\boxed{60}
$$

# 29. Literal Equations

A **literal equation** contains multiple variables.

Example:

$$
d=rt
$$

You may need to solve for a particular variable.

Solve for $t$:

$$
d=rt
$$

Divide both sides by $r$:

$$
\boxed{t=\frac{d}{r}}
$$

---

## Example: Solve for $r$

Starting with:

$$
d=rt
$$

Divide both sides by $t$:

$$
\boxed{r=\frac{d}{t}}
$$

# 30. The Slope Formula

The slope between two points is:

$$
m=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}
$$

Slope measures the rate of change.

For example, given:

$$
(2,3)
$$

and:

$$
(5,9)
$$

the slope is:

$$
m=\frac{9-3}{5-2}
$$

$$
m=\frac{6}{3}
$$

$$
\boxed{m=2}
$$

---

# 31. Linear Equations in Slope-Intercept Form

The slope-intercept form is:

$$
\boxed{y=mx+b}
$$

where:

* $m$ = slope
* $b$ = $y$-intercept

Example:

$$
y=3x+2
$$

The slope is:

$$
\boxed{3}
$$

The $y$-intercept is:

$$
\boxed{2}
$$

---

# 32. Solving for $y$

Consider:

$$
2x+y=8
$$

Subtract $2x$ from both sides:

$$
y=8-2x
$$

Rewrite:

$$
\boxed{y=-2x+8}
$$

The slope is:

$$
-2
$$

The $y$-intercept is:

$$
8
$$

---

# 33. Standard Form

A linear equation can also be written in standard form:

$$
\boxed{Ax+By=C}
$$

where $A$, $B$, and $C$ are constants.

Example:

$$
2x+3y=12
$$

This is in standard form.

---

# 34. Graphing a Linear Equation

To graph:

$$
y=2x+1
$$

Start with the $y$-intercept:

$$
(0,1)
$$

The slope is:

$$
2=\frac{2}{1}
$$

This means:

* Rise 2
* Run 1

From $(0,1)$, move up 2 and right 1.

This gives another point:

$$
(1,3)
$$

Connect the points with a straight line.

---

# 35. Linear Equations and Tables

Consider:

$$
y=2x+3
$$

| $x$ | $y$ |
| --- | --- |
| 0   | 3   |
| 1   | 5   |
| 2   | 7   |
| 3   | 9   |

The output increases by 2 whenever $x$ increases by 1.

Therefore, the slope is:

$$
\boxed{2}
$$

---

# 36. Linear Equations and Graphs

A graph represents a linear equation if it forms a straight line.

Examples of linear relationships:

* Constant hourly pay
* Distance traveled at a constant speed
* Fixed fee plus a constant rate
* Simple cost relationships

The slope represents the rate of change.

The $y$-intercept represents the initial value.

---

# 37. Comparing Linear Equations

Suppose:

$$
y=3x+5
$$

and:

$$
y=2x+8
$$

The first equation has a greater slope:

$$
3>2
$$

Therefore, its value increases faster.

---

# 38. Systems of Linear Equations

A system contains two or more equations.

Example:

$$
y=2x+1
$$

$$
y=x+4
$$

The solution is the point where the two lines intersect.

Set the equations equal:

$$
2x+1=x+4
$$

Subtract $x$:

$$
x+1=4
$$

Subtract 1:

$$
x=3
$$

Substitute:

$$
y=3+4=7
$$

The solution is:

$$
\boxed{(3,7)}
$$

---

# 39. Common Mistakes

## Mistake 1: Doing Different Operations on Each Side

Incorrect:

$$
x+5=12
$$

Subtract 5 from only one side.

Correct:

$$
x+5-5=12-5
$$

---

## Mistake 2: Forgetting to Distribute

Incorrect:

$$
3(x+4)=3x+4
$$

Correct:

$$
\boxed{3x+12}
$$

---

## Mistake 3: Sign Errors

Remember:

$$
-(-x)=+x
$$

and:

$$
(-)(-)=(+)
$$

---

## Mistake 4: Combining Unlike Terms

You cannot combine:

$$
3x+5
$$

into:

$$
8x
$$

The correct simplified expression is:

$$
\boxed{3x+5}
$$

---

## Mistake 5: Not Checking the Answer

Always substitute your answer into the original equation.

---

# 40. A Complete Problem-Solving Strategy

When solving a linear equation:

### Step 1: Simplify both sides.

Combine like terms and distribute.

### Step 2: Move variable terms to one side.

Use addition or subtraction.

### Step 3: Move constant terms to the other side.

Use addition or subtraction.

### Step 4: Isolate the variable.

Use multiplication or division.

### Step 5: Check your solution.

Substitute the answer into the original equation.

# Summary

A linear equation has variables with a maximum exponent of 1.

To solve linear equations:

1. Simplify both sides.
2. Use inverse operations.
3. Keep the equation balanced.
4. Isolate the variable.
5. Check your answer.

Important forms include:

### Slope-Intercept Form

$$
\boxed{y=mx+b}
$$

### Standard Form

$$
\boxed{Ax+By=C}
$$

### Point-Slope Form

$$
\boxed{y-y_1=m(x-x_1)}
$$

Linear equations are used to represent:

* Costs
* Rates
* Distance
* Age
* Perimeters
* Percentages
* Real-world relationships

The key idea is:

$$
\boxed{\text{An equation is a balance, and solving means finding the value that keeps it balanced.}}
$$
