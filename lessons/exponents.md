# GED Math Lesson: Exponents

## Learning Goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

* Identify the **base** and **exponent** in an expression.
* Evaluate powers.
* Use the zero and first-power rules.
* Apply exponent rules when multiplying and dividing powers.
* Simplify a power raised to another power.
* Apply exponents to real-world problems.

---

# 1. What Is an Exponent?

An **exponent** tells you how many times to multiply a number by itself.

In the expression:

$$
a^n
$$

* $a$ is the **base**.
* $n$ is the **exponent** or **power**.

For example:

$$
3^4
$$

The base is $3$.

The exponent is $4$.

This means that 3 is used as a factor four times:

$$
3^4 = 3 \times 3 \times 3 \times 3
$$

$$
3^4 = 81
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{3^4 = 81}
$$

---

# 2. Evaluating Exponents

To evaluate an exponent, multiply the base by itself the number of times shown by the exponent.

---

## Example 1

Evaluate:

$$
2^5
$$

Expand the exponent:

$$
2^5 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2
$$

Solve:

$$
2^5 = 32
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{2^5 = 32}
$$

---

## Example 2

Evaluate:

$$
5^2
$$

$$
5^2 = 5 \times 5
$$

$$
5^2 = 25
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{5^2 = 25}
$$

---

## Example 3

Evaluate:

$$
10^3
$$

$$
10^3 = 10 \times 10 \times 10
$$

$$
10^3 = 1000
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{10^3 = 1000}
$$

---

# 3. Special Exponents

Two exponent rules are especially important.

---

## Any Number to the First Power

Any number raised to the first power equals itself.

$$
a^1 = a
$$

### Example

$$
9^1 = 9
$$

$$
125^1 = 125
$$

---

## Any Nonzero Number to the Zero Power

Any nonzero number raised to the zero power equals 1.

$$
a^0 = 1
$$

where:

$$
a \neq 0
$$

### Examples

$$
8^0 = 1
$$

$$
125^0 = 1
$$

$$
(-7)^0 = 1
$$

> **Important:** This rule applies to nonzero numbers. The expression $0^0$ is not treated as 1 in ordinary arithmetic.

---

# 4. Squaring and Cubing

Some powers have special names.

---

## Squaring

An exponent of 2 is called **squared**.

$$
6^2 = 36
$$

Read this as:

> Six squared.

---

## Cubing

An exponent of 3 is called **cubed**.

$$
4^3 = 64
$$

Read this as:

> Four cubed.

---

# 5. Multiplying Powers with the Same Base

When multiplying powers with the **same base**, add the exponents.

## Rule

$$
a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}
$$

### Example

Simplify:

$$
2^3 \times 2^4
$$

The bases are the same, so add the exponents:

$$
2^{3+4}
$$

$$
2^7
$$

Evaluate:

$$
2^7 = 128
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{2^3 \times 2^4 = 128}
$$

### Why Does This Work?

$$
2^3 \times 2^4
$$

means:

$$
(2 \times 2 \times 2)(2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2)
$$

There are seven factors of 2:

$$
2^7
$$

---

# 6. Dividing Powers with the Same Base

When dividing powers with the **same base**, subtract the exponents.

## Rule

$$
\frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}
$$

where $a \neq 0$.

### Example

Simplify:

$$
\frac{5^6}{5^2}
$$

Subtract the exponents:

$$
5^{6-2}
$$

$$
5^4
$$

Evaluate:

$$
5^4 = 625
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\frac{5^6}{5^2} = 625}
$$

---

# 7. Power of a Power

When raising a power to another power, multiply the exponents.

## Rule

$$
(a^m)^n = a^{mn}
$$

### Example

Simplify:

$$
(3^2)^4
$$

Multiply the exponents:

$$
3^{2 \times 4}
$$

$$
3^8
$$

Evaluate:

$$
3^8 = 6561
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{(3^2)^4 = 6561}
$$

---

# 8. Negative Exponents

A negative exponent means the reciprocal of the positive power.

## Rule

$$
a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}
$$

### Example

$$
2^{-3}
$$

Rewrite using a positive exponent:

$$
2^{-3} = \frac{1}{2^3}
$$

Evaluate:

$$
\frac{1}{2^3} = \frac{1}{8}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{2^{-3} = \frac{1}{8}}
$$

---

# 9. Exponents in Word Problems

Exponents are often used to represent repeated multiplication or growth.

### Example

A bacteria population doubles every hour.

The population starts with 4 bacteria.

How many bacteria will there be after 5 hours?

The population doubles 5 times:

$$
4 \times 2^5
$$

Evaluate the exponent:

$$
2^5 = 32
$$

Now multiply:

$$
4 \times 32 = 128
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{128\text{ bacteria}}
$$

---

# 10. Exponent Rules Reference

| Situation                            | Rule                        |
| ------------------------------------ | --------------------------- |
| Any number to the first power        | $a^1 = a$                   |
| Any nonzero number to the zero power | $a^0 = 1$                   |
| Multiply same bases                  | $a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}$  |
| Divide same bases                    | $\frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}$ |
| Power of a power                     | $(a^m)^n = a^{mn}$          |
| Negative exponent                    | $a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}$    |

---

# GED Tips

* The **base** is the number being multiplied.
* The **exponent** tells how many times the base is used as a factor.
* Multiplying powers with the same base → **add exponents**.
* Dividing powers with the same base → **subtract exponents**.
* Raising a power to another power → **multiply exponents**.
* A nonzero number raised to the zero power equals **1**.
* A negative exponent creates a **reciprocal**.

---

# Memory Trick

Remember:

## Same Base

### Multiply → Add

$$
a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}
$$

### Divide → Subtract

$$
\frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}
$$

### Power of a Power → Multiply

$$
(a^m)^n = a^{mn}
$$

A simple memory trick is:

> **Multiplication adds. Division subtracts. Powers multiply.**
