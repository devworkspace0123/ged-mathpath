# Quadratic Equations

## Learning Goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

* Identify quadratic equations.
* Understand the standard form of a quadratic equation.
* Solve quadratic equations by factoring.
* Solve quadratic equations using the square root method.
* Solve quadratic equations using the quadratic formula.
* Complete the square.
* Identify the discriminant.
* Determine the number of solutions.
* Understand the relationship between quadratic equations and their graphs.
* Find the vertex and axis of symmetry.
* Solve real-world problems involving quadratic equations.

---

# 1. What Is a Quadratic Equation?

A **quadratic equation** is an equation in which the highest exponent of the variable is 2.

The standard form is:

$$
\boxed{ax^2+bx+c=0}
$$

where:

$$
a\ne0
$$

The letters $a$, $b$, and $c$ are constants.

---

## Examples

$$
x^2+5x+6=0
$$

$$
2x^2-3x+1=0
$$

$$
x^2-16=0
$$

All of these are quadratic equations.

---

# 2. Identifying $a$, $b$, and $c$

Consider:

$$
3x^2+7x-4=0
$$

Compare it with:

$$
ax^2+bx+c=0
$$

Therefore:

$$
a=3
$$

$$
b=7
$$

$$
c=-4
$$

---

## Example

For:

$$
x^2-9x+20=0
$$

we have:

$$
a=1,\quad b=-9,\quad c=20
$$

Remember:

> If no number is written before $x^2$, the coefficient is 1.

---

# 3. Quadratic Functions

A quadratic function has the form:

$$
\boxed{y=ax^2+bx+c}
$$

Its graph is called a:

$$
\boxed{\text{parabola}}
$$

For example:

$$
y=x^2
$$

is a quadratic function.

---

# 4. The Graph of a Quadratic Function

A parabola can open:

### Upward

when:

$$
a>0
$$

Example:

$$
y=x^2
$$

### Downward

when:

$$
a<0
$$

Example:

$$
y=-x^2
$$

---

# 5. The Vertex

The **vertex** is the highest or lowest point of a parabola.

For:

$$
y=ax^2+bx+c
$$

the $x$-coordinate of the vertex is:

$$
\boxed{x=\frac{-b}{2a}}
$$

Then substitute this value into the equation to find the $y$-coordinate.

---

## Example

Find the vertex of:

$$
y=x^2-4x+3
$$

Here:

$$
a=1,\quad b=-4
$$

Use:

$$
x=\frac{-b}{2a}
$$

$$
x=\frac{-(-4)}{2(1)}
$$

$$
x=2
$$

Substitute $x=2$:

$$
y=2^2-4(2)+3
$$

$$
y=4-8+3
$$

$$
y=-1
$$

The vertex is:

$$
\boxed{(2,-1)}
$$

---

# 6. The Axis of Symmetry

The axis of symmetry is a vertical line passing through the vertex.

Its equation is:

$$
\boxed{x=\frac{-b}{2a}}
$$

For:

$$
y=x^2-4x+3
$$

the axis of symmetry is:

$$
\boxed{x=2}
$$

---

# 7. Solving Quadratic Equations

There are several methods for solving quadratic equations:

1. Factoring
2. Square root method
3. Completing the square
4. Quadratic formula

---

# 8. Solving by Factoring

Consider:

$$
x^2+5x+6=0
$$

Find two numbers that:

* Multiply to $6$.
* Add to $5$.

The numbers are:

$$
2\text{ and }3
$$

Therefore:

$$
x^2+5x+6=(x+2)(x+3)
$$

So:

$$
(x+2)(x+3)=0
$$

---

# 9. The Zero Product Property

If:

$$
AB=0
$$

then:

$$
A=0
$$

or:

$$
B=0
$$

Therefore:

$$
x+2=0
$$

or:

$$
x+3=0
$$

Solve:

$$
x=-2
$$

or:

$$
x=-3
$$

The solutions are:

$$
\boxed{x=-2,\,-3}
$$

---

# 10. Factoring a Difference of Squares

A difference of squares has the form:

$$
a^2-b^2
$$

The formula is:

$$
\boxed{a^2-b^2=(a-b)(a+b)}
$$

---

## Example

Solve:

$$
x^2-25=0
$$

Rewrite:

$$
x^2-5^2=0
$$

Factor:

$$
(x-5)(x+5)=0
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{x=5\text{ or }x=-5}
$$

---

# 11. Factoring Quadratics with a Leading Coefficient of 1

Consider:

$$
x^2+7x+12=0
$$

Find two numbers that:

* Multiply to 12.
* Add to 7.

The numbers are:

$$
3\text{ and }4
$$

Therefore:

$$
(x+3)(x+4)=0
$$

Solutions:

$$
\boxed{x=-3,\,-4}
$$

---

# 12. Factoring Quadratics with a Leading Coefficient Greater Than 1

Consider:

$$
2x^2+7x+3=0
$$

We need two numbers that multiply to:

$$
2\cdot3=6
$$

and add to:

$$
7
$$

The numbers are:

$$
6\text{ and }1
$$

Rewrite the middle term:

$$
2x^2+6x+x+3=0
$$

Group:

$$
2x(x+3)+1(x+3)=0
$$

Factor:

$$
(2x+1)(x+3)=0
$$

Therefore:

$$
2x+1=0
$$

or:

$$
x+3=0
$$

Solutions:

$$
\boxed{x=-\frac12,\,-3}
$$

---

# 13. Solving by Taking Square Roots

Use this method when the equation has the form:

$$
x^2=k
$$

Take the square root of both sides:

$$
x=\pm\sqrt{k}
$$

The symbol $\pm$ means:

> positive or negative.

---

## Example

Solve:

$$
x^2=25
$$

Take the square root:

$$
x=\pm\sqrt{25}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{x=5\text{ or }x=-5}
$$

---

# 14. Example with a Coefficient

Solve:

$$
3x^2=27
$$

Divide by 3:

$$
x^2=9
$$

Take the square root:

$$
\boxed{x=\pm3}
$$

---

# 15. Completing the Square

Completing the square transforms a quadratic into a perfect square.

Consider:

$$
x^2+6x+5=0
$$

Move the constant:

$$
x^2+6x=-5
$$

Take half of 6:

$$
\frac{6}{2}=3
$$

Square it:

$$
3^2=9
$$

Add 9 to both sides:

$$
x^2+6x+9=4
$$

Factor:

$$
(x+3)^2=4
$$

Take the square root:

$$
x+3=\pm2
$$

Therefore:

$$
x=-3\pm2
$$

Solutions:

$$
\boxed{x=-1\text{ or }x=-5}
$$

---

# 16. The Quadratic Formula

The quadratic formula solves any quadratic equation in standard form:

$$
ax^2+bx+c=0
$$

The formula is:

$$
\boxed{x=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}}
$$

---

# 17. Using the Quadratic Formula

Solve:

$$
x^2+5x+6=0
$$

Identify:

$$
a=1,\quad b=5,\quad c=6
$$

Substitute:

$$
x=
\frac{-5\pm\sqrt{5^2-4(1)(6)}}{2(1)}
$$

Simplify:

$$
x=
\frac{-5\pm\sqrt{25-24}}{2}
$$

$$
x=
\frac{-5\pm1}{2}
$$

First solution:

$$
x=\frac{-5+1}{2}=-2
$$

Second solution:

$$
x=\frac{-5-1}{2}=-3
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{x=-2,\,-3}
$$

---

# 18. The Discriminant

The expression under the square root in the quadratic formula is called the **discriminant**.

$$
\boxed{b^2-4ac}
$$

The discriminant tells you how many real solutions a quadratic equation has.

---

# 19. Positive Discriminant

If:

$$
b^2-4ac>0
$$

there are:

$$
\boxed{2\text{ real solutions}}
$$

The graph crosses the $x$-axis twice.

---

# 20. Zero Discriminant

If:

$$
b^2-4ac=0
$$

there is:

$$
\boxed{1\text{ real solution}}
$$

The graph touches the $x$-axis once.

---

# 21. Negative Discriminant

If:

$$
b^2-4ac<0
$$

there are:

$$
\boxed{0\text{ real solutions}}
$$

The graph does not cross the $x$-axis.

---

# 22. Quadratic Equations and $x$-Intercepts

The solutions of:

$$
ax^2+bx+c=0
$$

are the $x$-intercepts of:

$$
y=ax^2+bx+c
$$

For example:

$$
y=x^2-5x+6
$$

Set $y=0$:

$$
x^2-5x+6=0
$$

Factor:

$$
(x-2)(x-3)=0
$$

The $x$-intercepts are:

$$
\boxed{(2,0)\text{ and }(3,0)}
$$

---

# 23. The $y$-Intercept

For:

$$
y=ax^2+bx+c
$$

the $y$-intercept is:

$$
\boxed{c}
$$

because when:

$$
x=0
$$

the equation becomes:

$$
y=c
$$

---

# 24. Vertex Form

A quadratic can be written in vertex form:

$$
\boxed{y=a(x-h)^2+k}
$$

The vertex is:

$$
\boxed{(h,k)}
$$

---

## Example

$$
y=2(x-3)^2+4
$$

The vertex is:

$$
\boxed{(3,4)}
$$

The parabola opens upward because:

$$
a=2>0
$$

---

# 25. Standard Form vs. Vertex Form

### Standard Form

$$
y=ax^2+bx+c
$$

### Vertex Form

$$
y=a(x-h)^2+k
$$

Standard form is useful for:

* Finding the $y$-intercept.
* Using the quadratic formula.

Vertex form is useful for:

* Finding the vertex.
* Understanding the maximum or minimum.

---

# 26. Maximum and Minimum Values

If:

$$
a>0
$$

the parabola opens upward and has a:

$$
\boxed{\text{minimum}}
$$

If:

$$
a<0
$$

the parabola opens downward and has a:

$$
\boxed{\text{maximum}}
$$

---

# 27. Quadratic Word Problems

Quadratic equations appear in many real-world situations.

Examples include:

* Area
* Projectile motion
* Profit
* Revenue
* Product dimensions
* Maximum and minimum values

---

# 28. Example: Area Problem

A rectangle has a width of $x$ and a length of $x+5$. Its area is 24 square units.

The area formula is:

$$
A=lw
$$

Substitute:

$$
24=x(x+5)
$$

Expand:

$$
24=x^2+5x
$$

Move everything to one side:

$$
x^2+5x-24=0
$$

Factor:

$$
(x+8)(x-3)=0
$$

Solutions:

$$
x=-8
$$

or:

$$
x=3
$$

A length cannot be negative, so:

$$
\boxed{x=3}
$$

The width is 3 units.

The length is:

$$
3+5=\boxed{8}
$$

---

# 29. Example: Projectile Motion

The height of a ball can be modeled by:

$$
h=-16t^2+64t+5
$$

where:

* $h$ = height
* $t$ = time

To find when the ball reaches the ground, set:

$$
h=0
$$

Therefore:

$$
-16t^2+64t+5=0
$$

This quadratic equation can be solved using the quadratic formula.

---

# 30. Choosing the Best Method

### Use factoring when:

The quadratic factors easily.

Example:

$$
x^2+5x+6=0
$$

### Use square roots when:

The equation contains a squared variable by itself.

Example:

$$
x^2=25
$$

### Use completing the square when:

You need vertex form or want to understand the structure of the quadratic.

### Use the quadratic formula when:

The quadratic does not factor easily.

The quadratic formula works for every quadratic equation.

---

# 31. Common Mistakes

## Mistake 1: Forgetting the $\pm$

When:

$$
x^2=25
$$

the answer is:

$$
\boxed{x=\pm5}
$$

not just:

$$
x=5
$$

---

## Mistake 2: Incorrect Signs in the Formula

The formula is:

$$
x=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}
$$

Pay attention to:

$$
-b
$$

and:

$$
-4ac
$$

---

## Mistake 3: Forgetting to Set the Equation Equal to Zero

Factoring requires:

$$
ax^2+bx+c=0
$$

For example:

$$
x^2+5x=6
$$

must become:

$$
x^2+5x-6=0
$$

before factoring.

---

## Mistake 4: Accepting an Impossible Answer

In real-world problems, negative values may not make sense.

For example:

* Negative length
* Negative time
* Negative number of objects

Always check the context.

---

# 32. A Reliable Strategy

When solving a quadratic equation:

1. Write the equation in standard form.
2. Identify $a$, $b$, and $c$ if necessary.
3. Choose a method:

   * Factoring
   * Square roots
   * Completing the square
   * Quadratic formula
4. Solve for all possible values.
5. Check your solutions.
6. Consider whether the answers make sense in context.

# Summary

A quadratic equation has the form:

$$
\boxed{ax^2+bx+c=0}
$$

where:

$$
a\ne0
$$

The main methods for solving quadratic equations are:

### Factoring

$$
(x+2)(x+3)=0
$$

### Square Roots

$$
x^2=25
$$

### Completing the Square

$$
(x-h)^2=k
$$

### Quadratic Formula

$$
\boxed{x=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}}
$$

The graph of a quadratic function is a parabola.

The key ideas are:

$$
\boxed{\text{Solutions of a quadratic equation are the }x\text{-intercepts of its graph.}}
$$

and:

$$
\boxed{\text{The vertex represents the maximum or minimum value.}}
$$
