# GED Math Lesson: Circles

## Radius, Diameter, Circumference, and Area

## Learning Goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

* Identify the **radius** and **diameter** of a circle.
* Convert between radius and diameter.
* Find the **circumference** of a circle.
* Find the **area** of a circle.
* Solve for radius or diameter when circumference is given.
* Find the area and perimeter of a semicircle.
* Solve GED-style circle problems.

---

# 1. Parts of a Circle

A circle has several important measurements.

---

## Radius

The **radius** is the distance from the center of a circle to any point on its edge.

The symbol for radius is:

$$
r
$$

---

## Diameter

The **diameter** is the distance across the entire circle through its center.

The symbol for diameter is:

$$
d
$$

The diameter is always twice the radius:

$$
d = 2r
$$

Therefore:

$$
r = \frac{d}{2}
$$

### Important Relationship

$$
\boxed{d = 2r}
$$

A diameter is always twice as long as the radius.

---

# 2. Circumference

The **circumference** is the distance around a circle.

Think of the circumference as the circular version of a perimeter.

---

## Circumference Formula Using the Radius

If the radius is given:

$$
C = 2\pi r
$$

where:

* $C$ = circumference
* $r$ = radius

---

## Circumference Formula Using the Diameter

If the diameter is given:

$$
C = \pi d
$$

where:

* $C$ = circumference
* $d$ = diameter

---

## Which Formula Should You Use?

| Given    | Formula      |
| -------- | ------------ |
| Radius   | $C = 2\pi r$ |
| Diameter | $C = \pi d$  |

Unless the problem gives you a different instruction, you can usually use:

$$
\pi \approx 3.14
$$

---

## Example 1: Circumference Using the Radius

A circle has a radius of **6 inches**.

Find the circumference. Use $\pi \approx 3.14$.

### Formula

$$
C = 2\pi r
$$

### Substitute

$$
C = 2(3.14)(6)
$$

### Solve

$$
C = 37.68
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{C \approx 37.68\text{ in}}
$$

---

## Example 2: Circumference Using the Diameter

A circle has a diameter of **14 cm**.

Find the circumference. Use $\pi \approx 3.14$.

### Formula

$$
C = \pi d
$$

### Substitute

$$
C = 3.14(14)
$$

### Solve

$$
C = 43.96
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{C \approx 43.96\text{ cm}}
$$

---

# 3. Area of a Circle

The **area** of a circle measures the amount of space inside the circle.

## Formula

$$
A = \pi r^2
$$

where:

* $A$ = area
* $r$ = radius

> **Important:** The area formula uses the radius, not the diameter.

If you are given the diameter, find the radius first:

$$
r = \frac{d}{2}
$$

---

## Example: Finding the Area

A circle has a radius of **5 feet**.

Find its area. Use $\pi \approx 3.14$.

### Formula

$$
A = \pi r^2
$$

### Substitute

$$
A = 3.14(5)^2
$$

### Evaluate the Exponent

$$
A = 3.14(25)
$$

### Solve

$$
A = 78.5
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{A = 78.5\text{ ft}^2}
$$

---

# 4. Finding the Radius from the Diameter

The relationship between diameter and radius is:

$$
d = 2r
$$

To find the radius, divide the diameter by 2:

$$
r = \frac{d}{2}
$$

---

## Example

The diameter of a circle is **18 inches**.

Find the radius.

### Formula

$$
r = \frac{d}{2}
$$

### Substitute

$$
r = \frac{18}{2}
$$

### Solve

$$
r = 9
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{r = 9\text{ in}}
$$

---

# 5. Finding the Diameter from the Radius

Use:

$$
d = 2r
$$

---

## Example

The radius of a circle is **12 cm**.

Find the diameter.

### Formula

$$
d = 2r
$$

### Substitute

$$
d = 2(12)
$$

### Solve

$$
d = 24
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{d = 24\text{ cm}}
$$

---

# 6. Finding the Radius from the Circumference

Start with the circumference formula:

$$
C = 2\pi r
$$

To solve for $r$, divide both sides by $2\pi$:

$$
r = \frac{C}{2\pi}
$$

---

## Example

The circumference of a circle is **31.4 cm**.

Find the radius. Use $\pi \approx 3.14$.

### Formula

$$
r = \frac{C}{2\pi}
$$

### Substitute

$$
r = \frac{31.4}{2(3.14)}
$$

### Solve

$$
r = \frac{31.4}{6.28}
$$

$$
r = 5
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{r = 5\text{ cm}}
$$

---

# 7. Finding the Diameter from the Circumference

Start with:

$$
C = \pi d
$$

To solve for $d$, divide both sides by $\pi$:

$$
d = \frac{C}{\pi}
$$

---

## Example

The circumference of a circle is **62.8 cm**.

Find the diameter. Use $\pi \approx 3.14$.

### Formula

$$
d = \frac{C}{\pi}
$$

### Substitute

$$
d = \frac{62.8}{3.14}
$$

### Solve

$$
d = 20
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{d = 20\text{ cm}}
$$

---

# 8. Semicircles

A **semicircle** is half of a circle.

Because a full circle is divided into two equal halves:

$$
\text{Semicircle} = \frac{1}{2}\text{ of a circle}
$$

---

## Area of a Semicircle

The area of a full circle is:

$$
A = \pi r^2
$$

Therefore, the area of a semicircle is:

$$
A = \frac{1}{2}\pi r^2
$$

---

## Curved Part of a Semicircle

The circumference of a full circle is:

$$
C = 2\pi r
$$

The curved part of a semicircle is half of the circumference:

$$
\frac{1}{2}(2\pi r) = \pi r
$$

---

## Perimeter of a Semicircle

The perimeter includes:

1. The curved half of the circle.
2. The straight diameter.

Therefore:

$$
P = \pi r + 2r
$$

> **Important:** Do not forget the straight diameter when finding the perimeter of a semicircle.

---

## Example: Perimeter of a Semicircle

A semicircle has a radius of **4 inches**.

Find its perimeter. Use $\pi \approx 3.14$.

### Formula

$$
P = \pi r + 2r
$$

### Substitute

$$
P = 3.14(4) + 2(4)
$$

### Solve

$$
P = 12.56 + 8
$$

$$
P = 20.56
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{P = 20.56\text{ in}}
$$

---

# 9. GED-Style Word Problem: Circumference

A circular flower garden has a radius of **9 feet**.

A fence will be placed around the entire garden.

How much fencing is needed? Use $\pi \approx 3.14$.

Because the fence goes **around** the garden, find the circumference.

### Formula

$$
C = 2\pi r
$$

### Substitute

$$
C = 2(3.14)(9)
$$

### Solve

$$
C = 56.52
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{56.52\text{ ft}}
$$

---

# 10. GED-Style Word Problem: Area

A circular table has a diameter of **12 feet**.

What is its area? Use $\pi \approx 3.14$.

The area formula requires the radius, so find the radius first.

### Step 1: Find the Radius

$$
r = \frac{d}{2}
$$

$$
r = \frac{12}{2}
$$

$$
r = 6
$$

### Step 2: Find the Area

Use:

$$
A = \pi r^2
$$

Substitute:

$$
A = 3.14(6)^2
$$

Evaluate the exponent:

$$
A = 3.14(36)
$$

Solve:

$$
A = 113.04
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{A = 113.04\text{ ft}^2}
$$

# 12. GED Tips

### Tip 1: Understand the Vocabulary

* **Radius** → center to edge
* **Diameter** → all the way across the circle
* **Circumference** → distance around the circle
* **Area** → space inside the circle

---

### Tip 2: Remember the Radius-Diameter Relationship

$$
d = 2r
$$

Therefore:

$$
r = \frac{d}{2}
$$

---

### Tip 3: Pay Attention to the Units

Circumference is a distance, so use ordinary units:

$$
\text{in},\ \text{ft},\ \text{cm},\ \text{m}
$$

Area uses square units:

$$
\text{in}^2,\ \text{ft}^2,\ \text{cm}^2,\ \text{m}^2
$$

---

### Tip 4: Look at the Wording

If the problem says:

* **around**
* **border**
* **fence**
* **distance around**

You probably need **circumference**.

If the problem says:

* **inside**
* **space covered**
* **surface**
* **how much material is needed to cover**

You probably need **area**.

---

### Tip 5: Be Careful with the Diameter

The area formula is:

$$
A = \pi r^2
$$

If the problem gives you the diameter, do not immediately square the diameter.

First find the radius:

$$
r = \frac{d}{2}
$$
