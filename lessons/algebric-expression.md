# Simplifying Linear Expressions

## Learning Goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

* Identify terms, coefficients, constants, and like terms.
* Add and subtract like terms.
* Multiply terms and expressions.
* Use the distributive property to expand expressions.
* Factor expressions by finding the greatest common factor.
* Simplify linear expressions accurately.
* Recognize common mistakes when working with algebraic expressions.

---

# 1. What Is a Linear Expression?

A **linear expression** is an algebraic expression in which the variable has an exponent of 1.

Examples:

$$
3x+5
$$

$$
-2x+7
$$

$$
4x-9+2x
$$

A linear expression does **not** contain:

$$
x^2,\quad x^3,\quad \sqrt{x}
$$

because these are not linear expressions.

---

# 2. Important Parts of an Expression

Consider:

$$
5x+8
$$

### Variable

The letter representing an unknown number.

$$
x
$$

### Coefficient

The number multiplying the variable.

$$
5x
$$

The coefficient is:

$$
5
$$

### Constant

A number without a variable.

$$
8
$$

### Term

A number, variable, or product of numbers and variables separated by addition or subtraction signs.

In:

$$
5x+8
$$

the terms are:

$$
5x \quad \text{and} \quad 8
$$

---

# 3. Like Terms

**Like terms** have exactly the same variables raised to the same powers.

### Like Terms

$$
3x \quad \text{and} \quad 7x
$$

$$
-5a \quad \text{and} \quad 2a
$$

$$
4x^2 \quad \text{and} \quad -9x^2
$$

### Unlike Terms

$$
3x \quad \text{and} \quad 3y
$$

$$
x \quad \text{and} \quad x^2
$$

$$
5x \quad \text{and} \quad 7
$$

You can combine **like terms**, but you cannot combine unlike terms.

---

# 4. Adding Like Terms

To add like terms:

1. Identify the like terms.
2. Add their coefficients.
3. Keep the variable part unchanged.

## Example

Simplify:

$$
3x+5x
$$

Both terms contain $x$, so they are like terms.

Add the coefficients:

$$
3+5=8
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{3x+5x=8x}
$$

---

## Example

Simplify:

$$
7a+2a-4a
$$

Combine the coefficients:

$$
7+2-4=5
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{7a+2a-4a=5a}
$$

---

# 5. Adding Expressions

Sometimes an expression contains both variable terms and constants.

Simplify:

$$
4x+7+3x+2
$$

### Step 1: Group like terms

$$
4x+3x+7+2
$$

### Step 2: Combine the variable terms

$$
4x+3x=7x
$$

### Step 3: Combine the constants

$$
7+2=9
$$

Final answer:

$$
\boxed{7x+9}
$$

---

# 6. Subtracting Like Terms

When subtracting terms, pay close attention to the signs.

Simplify:

$$
9x-4x
$$

Subtract the coefficients:

$$
9-4=5
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{9x-4x=5x}
$$

---

## Example

Simplify:

$$
8a-3a+2a
$$

Combine the coefficients:

$$
8-3+2=7
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{8a-3a+2a=7a}
$$

---

# 7. Subtracting an Expression

A common mistake occurs when a negative sign is outside parentheses.

Simplify:

$$
7x+5-(2x+3)
$$

The negative sign must be distributed to every term inside the parentheses:

$$
7x+5-2x-3
$$

Now combine like terms:

$$
7x-2x=5x
$$

$$
5-3=2
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{5x+2}
$$

---

# 8. Multiplying Terms

When multiplying numbers and variables:

1. Multiply the coefficients.
2. Multiply the variables.

## Example

Simplify:

$$
3x\cdot4
$$

Multiply the coefficients:

$$
3\cdot4=12
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{12x}
$$

---

## Example

Simplify:

$$
(3x)(2x)
$$

Multiply the coefficients:

$$
3\cdot2=6
$$

Multiply the variables:

$$
x\cdot x=x^2
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{6x^2}
$$

---

# 9. The Distributive Property

The distributive property allows you to multiply a number by every term inside parentheses.

The general rule is:

$$
a(b+c)=ab+ac
$$

For subtraction:

$$
a(b-c)=ab-ac
$$

Think of the outside factor as being distributed to **every term inside the parentheses**.

---

## Example

Expand:

$$
3(x+4)
$$

Multiply $3$ by both terms:

$$
3(x)+3(4)
$$

Simplify:

$$
\boxed{3x+12}
$$

---

## Example

Expand:

$$
-2(3x-5)
$$

Distribute $-2$:

$$
(-2)(3x)+(-2)(-5)
$$

Simplify:

$$
\boxed{-6x+10}
$$

### Important:

A negative times a negative is positive:

$$
(-2)(-5)=+10
$$

---

# 10. Simplifying After Distributing

Sometimes you must distribute first and then combine like terms.

Simplify:

$$
2(3x+4)+5x
$$

### Step 1: Distribute

$$
2(3x)+2(4)+5x
$$

$$
6x+8+5x
$$

### Step 2: Combine like terms

$$
6x+5x=11x
$$

Final answer:

$$
\boxed{11x+8}
$$

---

# 11. Expanding Expressions

**Expanding** means removing parentheses by using multiplication and the distributive property.

Example:

$$
4(x+3)
$$

Expanded form:

$$
\boxed{4x+12}
$$

Another example:

$$
-3(2x-7)
$$

Distribute:

$$
-3(2x)+(-3)(-7)
$$

Final answer:

$$
\boxed{-6x+21}
$$

---

# 12. Factoring Expressions

**Factoring** is the opposite of expanding.

Expanding:

$$
3(x+4)=3x+12
$$

Factoring:

$$
3x+12=3(x+4)
$$

To factor an expression:

1. Find the greatest common factor (GCF).
2. Divide each term by the GCF.
3. Write the GCF outside parentheses.

---

# 13. Factoring Out the Greatest Common Factor

Factor:

$$
6x+12
$$

### Step 1: Find the GCF

The greatest common factor of $6x$ and $12$ is:

$$
6
$$

### Step 2: Divide each term by 6

$$
6x\div6=x
$$

$$
12\div6=2
$$

### Step 3: Write the factored expression

$$
\boxed{6(x+2)}
$$

---

## Example

Factor:

$$
8x+20
$$

The GCF of $8$ and $20$ is $4$.

$$
8x\div4=2x
$$

$$
20\div4=5
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{8x+20=4(2x+5)}
$$

---

# 14. Factoring with a Negative GCF

Factor:

$$
-6x+12
$$

You can factor out $-6$:

$$
-6x\div(-6)=x
$$

$$
12\div(-6)=-2
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{-6(x-2)}
$$

Always check your answer by distributing:

$$
-6(x-2)=-6x+12
$$

---

# 15. Expanding and Factoring Are Opposites

These two processes undo each other.

### Expanding

$$
5(x+3)
$$

$$
=5x+15
$$

### Factoring

$$
5x+15
$$

$$
=5(x+3)
$$

A useful way to remember this:

> **Expanding removes parentheses. Factoring creates parentheses.**

---

# 16. A Complete Simplification Example

Simplify:

$$
3(2x+4)-x+5
$$

### Step 1: Distribute

$$
3(2x)+3(4)-x+5
$$

$$
6x+12-x+5
$$

### Step 2: Combine like terms

Variable terms:

$$
6x-x=5x
$$

Constants:

$$
12+5=17
$$

Final answer:

$$
\boxed{5x+17}
$$

---

# 17. Another Complete Example

Simplify:

$$
4(2x-3)+3(x+5)
$$

### Step 1: Distribute

$$
8x-12+3x+15
$$

### Step 2: Combine like terms

$$
8x+3x=11x
$$

$$
-12+15=3
$$

Final answer:

$$
\boxed{11x+3}
$$

---

# 18. Common Mistakes

## Mistake 1: Combining Unlike Terms

Incorrect:

$$
3x+5=8x
$$

The correct expression is:

$$
\boxed{3x+5}
$$

The terms $3x$ and $5$ are unlike terms.

---

## Mistake 2: Distributing to Only One Term

Incorrect:

$$
3(x+4)=3x+4
$$

The correct answer is:

$$
\boxed{3x+12}
$$

The $3$ must multiply both terms.

---

## Mistake 3: Forgetting Negative Signs

Incorrect:

$$
-2(x-5)=-2x-10
$$

Correct:

$$
-2(x-5)=-2x+10
$$

because:

$$
(-2)(-5)=+10
$$

---

## Mistake 4: Factoring the Wrong Number

When factoring, always look for the **greatest common factor**.

For example:

$$
12x+18
$$

The GCF is $6$, not $2$.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{12x+18=6(2x+3)}
$$

---

# 19. A Reliable Strategy for Simplifying Expressions

When you see a complicated expression, follow these steps:

### Step 1: Remove parentheses

Use the distributive property.

### Step 2: Rewrite the expression

Write all terms clearly.

### Step 3: Group like terms

Put variable terms together and constants together.

### Step 4: Combine like terms

Add or subtract the coefficients.

### Step 5: Check your answer

Make sure:

* Like terms have been combined.
* No unnecessary parentheses remain.
* Signs are correct.
* The expression is in a clear, simplified form.

# GED Test Tip

On the GED Math test, you may need to:

* Simplify an algebraic expression.
* Select an equivalent expression.
* Expand an expression.
* Factor an expression.
* Interpret an expression from a real-world situation.

Remember:

> **You can only combine like terms.**

For example:

$$
4x+3x=7x
$$

but:

$$
4x+3
$$

cannot be simplified further.

Also remember:

> **Distribute before combining like terms.**

For example:

$$
2(x+5)+3x
$$

First distribute:

$$
2x+10+3x
$$

Then combine:

$$
\boxed{5x+10}
$$

---

# Summary

To simplify linear expressions:

* **Add** the coefficients of like terms.
* **Subtract** the coefficients of like terms.
* **Multiply** coefficients and variables carefully.
* **Expand** by using the distributive property.
* **Factor** by taking out the greatest common factor.
* Always pay attention to **negative signs**.
* Remember that **expanding and factoring are opposite processes**.

The most important rule is:

$$
\boxed{\text{Only combine like terms.}}
$$
