# GED Math Lesson: Perimeter and Area

## Learning Goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

* Find the **perimeter** of common two-dimensional shapes.
* Find the **area** of common two-dimensional shapes.
* Choose the correct formula for a problem.
* Use the correct units for perimeter and area.
* Solve GED-style perimeter and area word problems.

---

## 1. Perimeter vs. Area

Before solving a problem, determine whether you need to find the **perimeter** or the **area**.

### Perimeter

The **perimeter** is the total distance around the outside of a shape.

Think of building a fence around a yard.

$$
\text{Perimeter} = \text{distance around the shape}
$$

Perimeter is measured in ordinary units such as:

* inches (in)
* feet (ft)
* centimeters (cm)
* meters (m)

---

### Area

The **area** is the amount of space inside a two-dimensional shape.

Think of covering a floor with carpet.

$$
\text{Area} = \text{space inside the shape}
$$

Area is measured in **square units**, such as:

* square inches ($\text{in}^2$)
* square feet ($\text{ft}^2$)
* square centimeters ($\text{cm}^2$)
* square meters ($\text{m}^2$)

---

## 2. Squares

A square has **four equal sides**.

Let $s$ represent the length of one side.

### Perimeter of a Square

#### Formula

$$
P = 4s
$$

### Area of a Square

#### Formula

$$
A = s^2
$$

### Example

A square has a side length of **7 cm**.

#### Find the Perimeter

**Formula:**

$$
P = 4s
$$

**Substitute:**

$$
P = 4(7)
$$

**Solve:**

$$
P = 28
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{P = 28\text{ cm}}
$$

#### Find the Area

**Formula:**

$$
A = s^2
$$

**Substitute:**

$$
A = 7^2
$$

**Solve:**

$$
A = 49
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{A = 49\text{ cm}^2}
$$

---

## 3. Rectangles

A rectangle has **two pairs of equal opposite sides**.

Let:

* $l$ = length
* $w$ = width

### Perimeter of a Rectangle

#### Formula

$$
P = 2l + 2w
$$

You can also use:

$$
P = 2(l+w)
$$

### Area of a Rectangle

#### Formula

$$
A = lw
$$

### Example

A rectangle has a length of **9 m** and a width of **6 m**.

#### Find the Perimeter

**Formula:**

$$
P = 2l + 2w
$$

**Substitute:**

$$
P = 2(9) + 2(6)
$$

**Solve:**

$$
P = 18 + 12 = 30
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{P = 30\text{ m}}
$$

#### Find the Area

**Formula:**

$$
A = lw
$$

**Substitute:**

$$
A = 9(6)
$$

**Solve:**

$$
A = 54
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{A = 54\text{ m}^2}
$$

---

## 4. Triangles

A triangle has **three sides**.

### Perimeter of a Triangle

To find the perimeter, add all three side lengths.

$$
P = a + b + c
$$

### Example

A triangle has side lengths of **6 cm**, **8 cm**, and **10 cm**.

$$
P = 6 + 8 + 10
$$

$$
P = 24
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{P = 24\text{ cm}}
$$

---

### Area of a Triangle

#### Formula

$$
A = \frac{1}{2}bh
$$

where:

* $b$ = base
* $h$ = height

> **Important:** The height must be perpendicular to the base.

### Example

A triangle has a base of **12 ft** and a height of **7 ft**.

**Formula:**

$$
A = \frac{1}{2}bh
$$

**Substitute:**

$$
A = \frac{1}{2}(12)(7)
$$

**Solve:**

$$
A = 42
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{A = 42\text{ ft}^2}
$$

---

## 5. Parallelograms

A parallelogram has **two pairs of parallel sides**.

Opposite sides are equal.

Let:

* $a$ and $b$ represent the side lengths.
* $b$ represent the base.
* $h$ represent the perpendicular height.

### Perimeter of a Parallelogram

#### Formula

$$
P = 2a + 2b
$$

### Area of a Parallelogram

#### Formula

$$
A = bh
$$

> **Important:** Use the perpendicular height, not the slanted side.

### Example

A parallelogram has a base of **10 in** and a height of **6 in**.

**Formula:**

$$
A = bh
$$

**Substitute:**

$$
A = 10(6)
$$

**Solve:**

$$
A = 60
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{A = 60\text{ in}^2}
$$

---

## 6. Trapezoids

A trapezoid has one pair of parallel sides.

The parallel sides are called the **bases**.

Let:

* $b_1$ = first base
* $b_2$ = second base
* $h$ = perpendicular height

### Area of a Trapezoid

#### Formula

$$
A = \frac{(b_1+b_2)h}{2}
$$

### Example

A trapezoid has bases of **10 cm** and **6 cm** and a height of **5 cm**.

**Formula:**

$$
A = \frac{(b_1+b_2)h}{2}
$$

**Substitute:**

$$
A = \frac{(10+6)(5)}{2}
$$

**Solve:**

$$
A = \frac{16(5)}{2}
$$

$$
A = \frac{80}{2}
$$

$$
A = 40
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{A = 40\text{ cm}^2}
$$

---

## 7. Circles

A circle can be described using its **radius** or **diameter**.

### Radius

The radius is the distance from the center of a circle to its edge.

### Diameter

The diameter is the distance across a circle through its center.

The relationship between the radius and diameter is:

$$
d = 2r
$$

Therefore:

$$
r = \frac{d}{2}
$$

---

## Circumference of a Circle

The circumference is the distance around a circle.

### Formulas

If the radius is given:

$$
C = 2\pi r
$$

If the diameter is given:

$$
C = \pi d
$$

### Example

A circle has a radius of **5 in**.

**Formula:**

$$
C = 2\pi r
$$

**Substitute:**

$$
C = 2\pi(5)
$$

$$
C = 10\pi
$$

Using:

$$
\pi \approx 3.14
$$

$$
C \approx 10(3.14)
$$

$$
C \approx 31.4
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{C \approx 31.4\text{ in}}
$$

---

## Area of a Circle

### Formula

$$
A = \pi r^2
$$

### Example

A circle has a radius of **4 ft**.

**Formula:**

$$
A = \pi r^2
$$

**Substitute:**

$$
A = \pi(4)^2
$$

$$
A = 16\pi
$$

Using:

$$
\pi \approx 3.14
$$

$$
A \approx 16(3.14)
$$

$$
A \approx 50.24
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{A \approx 50.24\text{ ft}^2}
$$

---

# 8. Formula Reference

| Shape         | Perimeter                   | Area                       |
| ------------- | --------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Square        | $P = 4s$                    | $A = s^2$                  |
| Rectangle     | $P = 2l + 2w$               | $A = lw$                   |
| Triangle      | $P = a+b+c$                 | $A = \frac{1}{2}bh$        |
| Parallelogram | $P = 2a+2b$                 | $A = bh$                   |
| Trapezoid     | Add all sides               | $A = \frac{(b_1+b_2)h}{2}$ |
| Circle        | $C = 2\pi r$ or $C = \pi d$ | $A = \pi r^2$              |

# 9. GED-Style Word Problems

## Example 1: Fencing a Garden

A rectangular garden measures **18 feet by 12 feet**.

How much fencing is needed to surround the garden?

Because fencing goes **around** the garden, find the perimeter.

**Formula:**

$$
P = 2l + 2w
$$

**Substitute:**

$$
P = 2(18) + 2(12)
$$

**Solve:**

$$
P = 36 + 24 = 60
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{60\text{ ft}}
$$

---

## Example 2: Covering a Floor

A classroom floor measures **30 feet by 24 feet**.

How many square feet of carpet are needed?

Because carpet covers the space **inside** the floor, find the area.

**Formula:**

$$
A = lw
$$

**Substitute:**

$$
A = 30(24)
$$

**Solve:**

$$
A = 720
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{720\text{ ft}^2}
$$

---

## Example 3: Area of a Circular Flower Bed

A circular flower bed has a radius of **7 feet**.

Find its area. Use $\pi \approx 3.14$.

**Formula:**

$$
A = \pi r^2
$$

**Substitute:**

$$
A = 3.14(7)^2
$$

**Solve:**

$$
A = 3.14(49)
$$

$$
A = 153.86
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{153.86\text{ ft}^2}
$$

---

## Example 4: Area of a Triangular Playground

A triangular playground has a base of **16 feet** and a height of **9 feet**.

Find its area.

**Formula:**

$$
A = \frac{1}{2}bh
$$

**Substitute:**

$$
A = \frac{1}{2}(16)(9)
$$

**Solve:**

$$
A = 72
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{72\text{ ft}^2}
$$

# 10. GED Tips

### Tip 1: Identify the Question

Ask yourself:

> Is the problem asking for the distance around the shape or the space inside it?

* Around → **Perimeter**
* Inside → **Area**

---

### Tip 2: Check the Units

Perimeter uses ordinary units:

$$
\text{cm},\ \text{m},\ \text{ft},\ \text{in}
$$

Area uses square units:

$$
\text{cm}^2,\ \text{m}^2,\ \text{ft}^2,\ \text{in}^2
$$

---

### Tip 3: Watch the Height

For triangles and parallelograms, the height must be **perpendicular** to the base.

Do not automatically use the slanted side.

---

### Tip 4: Check Radius and Diameter

Remember:

$$
d = 2r
$$

and:

$$
r = \frac{d}{2}
$$

If the problem gives the diameter but the formula requires the radius, divide the diameter by 2.

---

### Tip 5: Use the Correct Value of $\pi$

If the problem tells you to use a specific value of $\pi$, follow the instructions.

Otherwise, you may usually use:

$$
\pi \approx 3.14
$$

or leave the answer in terms of $\pi$ if appropriate.
