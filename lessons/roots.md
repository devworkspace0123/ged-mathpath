# GED Math Lesson: Squares, Square Roots, Cubes, and Cube Roots

## Goal

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

* Evaluate squares and cubes.
* Identify perfect squares and perfect cubes.
* Find square roots and cube roots.
* Simplify some square roots.
* Use roots to solve geometry and measurement problems.

---

# 1. Squaring a Number

To **square** a number, multiply it by itself.

$$
a^2=a\times a
$$

### Example

$$
5^2=5\times5=25
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{5^2=25}
$$

Another example:

$$
12^2=12\times12=144
$$

---

# 3. Square Roots

A **square root** asks:

> What number multiplied by itself gives this number?

The symbol is:

$$
\sqrt{}
$$

### Example

$$
\sqrt{49}
$$

Ask:

> What number times itself equals 49?

Since:

$$
7\times7=49
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\sqrt{49}=7}
$$

---

# 4. Squares and Square Roots Are Opposite Operations

Squaring and taking a square root undo each other.

For example:

$$
8^2=64
$$

and:

$$
\sqrt{64}=8
$$

This is why square roots are useful when solving equations such as:

$$
x^2=64
$$

For basic GED problems, the positive solution is:

$$
x=8
$$

---

# 5. Squaring Negative Numbers

Be careful with parentheses.

$$
(-3)^2=9
$$

because:

$$
(-3)(-3)=9
$$

However:

$$
-3^2=-9
$$

because the exponent applies to 3 before the negative sign.

This distinction is important when evaluating expressions.

---

# 6. Cubing a Number

To **cube** a number, multiply it by itself three times.

$$
a^3=a\times a\times a
$$

### Example

$$
3^3=3\times3\times3=27
$$

Another example:

$$
5^3=5\times5\times5=125
$$

---

# 8. Cube Roots

A **cube root** asks:

> What number multiplied by itself three times gives this number?

The symbol is:

$$
\sqrt[3]{}
$$

### Example

$$
\sqrt[3]{64}
$$

Since:

$$
4\times4\times4=64
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\sqrt[3]{64}=4}
$$

---

# 9. Roots in Geometry

Roots are often used to find missing dimensions.

### Example: Square

A square has an area of:

$$
196\text{ ft}^2
$$

The area formula is:

$$
A=s^2
$$

Therefore:

$$
s=\sqrt{196}
$$

$$
s=14
$$

The side length is:

$$
\boxed{14\text{ ft}}
$$

---

### Example: Cube

A cube has a volume of:

$$
216\text{ ft}^3
$$

The volume formula is:

$$
V=s^3
$$

Therefore:

$$
s=\sqrt[3]{216}
$$

$$
s=6
$$

The edge length is:

$$
\boxed{6\text{ ft}}
$$

---

# 10. Simplifying Square Roots

Sometimes a number inside a square root contains a perfect-square factor.

### Example

Simplify:

$$
\sqrt{8}
$$

Break 8 into:

$$
8=4\times2
$$

Therefore:

$$
\sqrt{8}=\sqrt{4\times2}
$$

Separate the factors:

$$
\sqrt4\sqrt2
$$

Since:

$$
\sqrt4=2
$$

we get:

$$
\boxed{\sqrt8=2\sqrt2}
$$

---

### Example

Simplify:

$$
\sqrt{18}
$$

Since:

$$
18=9\times2
$$

then:

$$
\sqrt{18}=\sqrt{9\times2}
$$

$$
=3\sqrt2
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\sqrt{18}=3\sqrt2}
$$

---

# 11. Adding and Subtracting Like Radicals

Radicals can be combined when they have the same value inside.

### Example

$$
5\sqrt7-2\sqrt7
$$

Subtract the coefficients:

$$
(5-2)\sqrt7
$$

$$
\boxed{3\sqrt7}
$$

---

### Example

$$
\sqrt8+\sqrt{18}
$$

Simplify each radical:

$$
\sqrt8=2\sqrt2
$$

$$
\sqrt{18}=3\sqrt2
$$

Now combine:

$$
2\sqrt2+3\sqrt2=5\sqrt2
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{5\sqrt2}
$$

---

### Important

You cannot combine:

$$
\sqrt2+\sqrt3
$$

because the radicals are not alike.

---

# 12. Multiplying Square Roots

Use:

$$
\sqrt a\cdot\sqrt b=\sqrt{ab}
$$

### Example

$$
\sqrt5\cdot\sqrt{20}
$$

$$
=\sqrt{100}
$$

$$
=10
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{10}
$$

---

# 13. Dividing Square Roots

Use:

$$
\frac{\sqrt a}{\sqrt b}
=
\sqrt{\frac ab}
$$

### Example

$$
\frac{\sqrt{81}}{\sqrt9}
$$

$$
=\sqrt{\frac{81}{9}}
$$

$$
=\sqrt9
$$

$$
=3
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{3}
$$

---

# GED Quick Reference

| Operation     | Meaning     |
| ------------- | ----------- |
| $a^2$         | $a$ squared |
| $a^3$         | $a$ cubed   |
| $\sqrt a$     | Square root |
| $\sqrt[3]{a}$ | Cube root   |

## Memory Trick

**Square → multiply 2 times**

$$
a^2=a\times a
$$

**Cube → multiply 3 times**

$$
a^3=a\times a\times a
$$

Roots are the **undo buttons**:

* Square root undoes a square.
* Cube root undoes a cube.
