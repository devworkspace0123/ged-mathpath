# GED Math Lesson: The Pythagorean Theorem

## Goal

Learn how to use the **Pythagorean Theorem** to find a missing side of a **right triangle** and solve real-world GED problems involving ladders, ramps, diagonals, and distances.

---

# 1. What Is the Pythagorean Theorem?

The **Pythagorean Theorem** is used to find a missing side of a **right triangle**.

A right triangle has one angle measuring exactly:

$$
90^\circ
$$

The theorem is:

$$
\boxed{a^2+b^2=c^2}
$$

where:

* $a$ and $b$ are the **legs**.
* $c$ is the **hypotenuse**.

---

# 2. The Parts of a Right Triangle

## The Legs

The two sides that form the $90^\circ$ angle are called the **legs**.

They are represented by:

$$
a
$$

and:

$$
b
$$

---

## The Hypotenuse

The side opposite the $90^\circ$ angle is the **hypotenuse**.

The hypotenuse is always:

* Opposite the right angle.
* The longest side.
* Represented by $c$.

### Important

The longest side is **not automatically** the hypotenuse unless the triangle is a right triangle.

---

# 3. Finding the Hypotenuse

When the hypotenuse is missing:

$$
c=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}
$$

### Example

A right triangle has legs measuring $6$ inches and $8$ inches. Find the hypotenuse.

Use:

$$
a^2+b^2=c^2
$$

Substitute:

$$
6^2+8^2=c^2
$$

Evaluate the squares:

$$
36+64=c^2
$$

$$
100=c^2
$$

Take the square root:

$$
c=\sqrt{100}=10
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{10\text{ inches}}
$$

---

# 4. Finding a Missing Leg

When a leg is missing, subtract.

Starting with:

$$
a^2+b^2=c^2
$$

If $b$ is missing:

$$
b^2=c^2-a^2
$$

Therefore:

$$
b=\sqrt{c^2-a^2}
$$

### Example

A right triangle has a hypotenuse of $17$ feet and one leg of $8$ feet. Find the other leg.

$$
8^2+b^2=17^2
$$

$$
64+b^2=289
$$

Subtract $64$:

$$
b^2=225
$$

Take the square root:

$$
b=\sqrt{225}=15
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{15\text{ feet}}
$$

---

# 5. Common Pythagorean Triples

A **Pythagorean triple** is a group of three whole numbers that satisfies:

$$
a^2+b^2=c^2
$$

Common triples include:

| Leg | Leg | Hypotenuse |
| --: | --: | ---------: |
|   3 |   4 |          5 |
|   5 |  12 |         13 |
|   6 |   8 |         10 |
|   8 |  15 |         17 |
|   7 |  24 |         25 |
|   9 |  12 |         15 |
|  12 |  16 |         20 |

For example:

$$
3^2+4^2=5^2
$$

$$
9+16=25
$$

These triples can save time on the GED.

---

# 6. Real-World Applications

The Pythagorean Theorem can be used to find:

* The height reached by a ladder.
* The length of a ramp.
* The diagonal of a rectangle.
* The shortest distance between two points.
* The length of a cable or support beam.

---

## Example: Ladder Problem

A ladder is $10$ feet long and its bottom is $6$ feet from a wall.

How high does the ladder reach?

The ladder is the hypotenuse.

$$
6^2+h^2=10^2
$$

$$
36+h^2=100
$$

$$
h^2=64
$$

$$
h=8
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{8\text{ feet}}
$$

---

# 7. Finding a Diagonal

A rectangle has a length of $12$ inches and a width of $5$ inches.

Find its diagonal.

The diagonal creates a right triangle:

$$
12^2+5^2=d^2
$$

$$
144+25=d^2
$$

$$
169=d^2
$$

$$
d=13
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{13\text{ inches}}
$$

---

# 8. How to Solve Any Pythagorean Theorem Problem

## If the Hypotenuse Is Missing

1. Identify the two legs.
2. Square both legs.
3. Add the squares.
4. Take the square root.

$$
c=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}
$$

---

## If a Leg Is Missing

1. Identify the hypotenuse.
2. Square the hypotenuse.
3. Square the known leg.
4. Subtract.
5. Take the square root.

$$
\text{Missing leg}
=
\sqrt{c^2-\text{known leg}^2}
$$

---

# Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Using the Theorem on Any Triangle

The Pythagorean Theorem works only for **right triangles**.

---

### Mistake 2: Choosing the Wrong Side as $c$

The hypotenuse is always opposite the $90^\circ$ angle.

---

### Mistake 3: Forgetting the Square Root

If you find:

$$
x^2=81
$$

the answer is:

$$
x=\sqrt{81}=9
$$

not $81$.

---

# GED Tips

* The formula is:

$$
\boxed{a^2+b^2=c^2}
$$

* $c$ is always the hypotenuse.
* Add when finding the hypotenuse.
* Subtract when finding a leg.
* The answer should usually be less than the hypotenuse when finding a leg.
* Check that your answer makes sense before finishing.

## Memory Trick

**Longest side = Hypotenuse.**

**Add to find $c$.**

**Subtract to find a leg.**

$$
\boxed{\text{Leg}^2+\text{Leg}^2=\text{Hypotenuse}^2}
$$
