# Operations with Polynomials

## Learning Goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

* Identify the terms, coefficients, and degree of a polynomial.
* Add polynomials by combining like terms.
* Subtract polynomials correctly.
* Multiply monomials and polynomials.
* Divide polynomials by monomials.
* Factor polynomials using the greatest common factor.
* Recognize common mistakes when performing polynomial operations.

---

# 1. What Is a Polynomial?

A **polynomial** is an algebraic expression made up of terms containing numbers and variables with nonnegative whole-number exponents.

Examples:

$$
3x+5
$$

$$
x^2+4x+4
$$

$$
2x^3-5x^2+x-7
$$

A polynomial can contain:

* Constants
* Variables
* Coefficients
* Addition and subtraction
* Whole-number exponents

---

# 2. Parts of a Polynomial

Consider the polynomial:

$$
4x^2-3x+7
$$

### Terms

The terms are:

$$
4x^2,\quad -3x,\quad 7
$$

### Coefficients

The coefficients are:

$$
4 \quad \text{and} \quad -3
$$

### Constant

The constant is:

$$
7
$$

### Degree

The highest exponent of the variable is the **degree**.

In:

$$
4x^2-3x+7
$$

the degree is:

$$
\boxed{2}
$$

---

# 3. Monomials, Binomials, and Trinomials

### Monomial

A polynomial with one term:

$$
5x
$$

$$
7x^2
$$

$$
-3
$$

### Binomial

A polynomial with two terms:

$$
x+4
$$

$$
3x^2-5x
$$

### Trinomial

A polynomial with three terms:

$$
x^2+3x+2
$$

---

# 4. Adding Polynomials

To add polynomials:

1. Remove parentheses.
2. Group like terms.
3. Combine the coefficients of like terms.

---

## Example

Add:

$$
(3x^2+4x+5)+(2x^2+7x+1)
$$

### Step 1: Remove parentheses

$$
3x^2+4x+5+2x^2+7x+1
$$

### Step 2: Combine like terms

$$
3x^2+2x^2=5x^2
$$

$$
4x+7x=11x
$$

$$
5+1=6
$$

Final answer:

$$
\boxed{5x^2+11x+6}
$$

---

# 5. Adding Polynomials Vertically

You can also arrange polynomials by matching like terms:

$$
\begin{array}{r}
3x^2+4x+5\\
+2x^2+7x+1\\
\hline
5x^2+11x+6
\end{array}
$$

Make sure that:

* $x^2$ terms are aligned.
* $x$ terms are aligned.
* Constants are aligned.

---

# 6. Subtracting Polynomials

When subtracting polynomials, distribute the negative sign to **every term** in the second polynomial.

---

## Example

Subtract:

$$
(5x^2+3x-4)-(2x^2-x+6)
$$

### Step 1: Distribute the negative sign

$$
5x^2+3x-4-2x^2+x-6
$$

Notice that every sign in the second polynomial changed.

### Step 2: Combine like terms

$$
5x^2-2x^2=3x^2
$$

$$
3x+x=4x
$$

$$
-4-6=-10
$$

Final answer:

$$
\boxed{3x^2+4x-10}
$$

---

# 7. Important Subtraction Rule

If there is a negative sign before parentheses:

$$
-(a+b-c)
$$

change the sign of every term:

$$
-a-b+c
$$

For example:

$$
-(3x^2-2x+5)
$$

becomes:

$$
\boxed{-3x^2+2x-5}
$$

---

# 8. Multiplying Monomials

To multiply monomials:

1. Multiply the coefficients.
2. Use the product rule for exponents.

The product rule is:

$$
x^a\cdot x^b=x^{a+b}
$$

---

## Example

Multiply:

$$
(3x^2)(4x^3)
$$

Multiply the coefficients:

$$
3\cdot4=12
$$

Add the exponents:

$$
x^2\cdot x^3=x^5
$$

Final answer:

$$
\boxed{12x^5}
$$

---

# 9. Multiplying a Monomial by a Polynomial

Use the distributive property.

## Example

Multiply:

$$
3x(2x^2+4x-5)
$$

Distribute $3x$ to every term:

$$
3x(2x^2)+3x(4x)+3x(-5)
$$

Multiply:

$$
6x^3+12x^2-15x
$$

Final answer:

$$
\boxed{6x^3+12x^2-15x}
$$

---

# 10. Multiplying Two Binomials

When multiplying two binomials, multiply every term in the first polynomial by every term in the second.

One method is **FOIL**:

* **F** — First
* **O** — Outer
* **I** — Inner
* **L** — Last

---

## Example

Multiply:

$$
(x+3)(x+5)
$$

### First

$$
x\cdot x=x^2
$$

### Outer

$$
x\cdot5=5x
$$

### Inner

$$
3\cdot x=3x
$$

### Last

$$
3\cdot5=15
$$

Combine:

$$
x^2+5x+3x+15
$$

$$
\boxed{x^2+8x+15}
$$

---

# 11. Multiplying a Binomial by a Binomial

Another example:

$$
(2x+3)(x-4)
$$

Multiply every term:

$$
2x(x)+2x(-4)+3(x)+3(-4)
$$

Simplify:

$$
2x^2-8x+3x-12
$$

Combine like terms:

$$
\boxed{2x^2-5x-12}
$$

---

# 12. Dividing Monomials

To divide monomials:

1. Divide the coefficients.
2. Subtract the exponents of like bases.

The quotient rule is:

$$
\frac{x^a}{x^b}=x^{a-b}
$$

---

## Example

Divide:

$$
\frac{12x^5}{3x^2}
$$

Divide the coefficients:

$$
12\div3=4
$$

Subtract the exponents:

$$
x^{5-2}=x^3
$$

Final answer:

$$
\boxed{4x^3}
$$

---

# 13. Dividing a Polynomial by a Monomial

Divide every term by the monomial.

## Example

Simplify:

$$
\frac{12x^3+8x^2-4x}{4x}
$$

Divide each term:

$$
\frac{12x^3}{4x}
+
\frac{8x^2}{4x}
-
\frac{4x}{4x}
$$

Simplify:

$$
\boxed{3x^2+2x-1}
$$

---

# 14. Factoring Polynomials

Factoring is the reverse of multiplication.

For example:

$$
3(x+4)=3x+12
$$

Factoring reverses this process:

$$
3x+12=3(x+4)
$$

---

# 15. Factoring Out the Greatest Common Factor

To factor a polynomial:

1. Find the greatest common factor (GCF).
2. Divide each term by the GCF.
3. Write the GCF outside parentheses.

---

## Example

Factor:

$$
6x^2+9x
$$

### Step 1: Find the GCF

The GCF of $6x^2$ and $9x$ is:

$$
3x
$$

### Step 2: Divide each term

$$
6x^2\div3x=2x
$$

$$
9x\div3x=3
$$

### Step 3: Write the factored form

$$
\boxed{3x(2x+3)}
$$

---

# 16. Factoring a Quadratic Trinomial

Some quadratic trinomials can be factored into two binomials.

Consider:

$$
x^2+5x+6
$$

We need two numbers that:

* Multiply to $6$.
* Add to $5$.

The numbers are:

$$
2 \quad \text{and} \quad 3
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{x^2+5x+6=(x+2)(x+3)}
$$

Check by multiplying:

$$
(x+2)(x+3)=x^2+5x+6
$$

---

# 17. Difference of Squares

A difference of squares has the form:

$$
a^2-b^2
$$

The factoring rule is:

$$
\boxed{a^2-b^2=(a-b)(a+b)}
$$

---

## Example

Factor:

$$
x^2-25
$$

Rewrite $25$ as $5^2$:

$$
x^2-5^2
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{(x-5)(x+5)}
$$

---

# 18. Factoring and Multiplying Are Opposites

Multiplication:

$$
(x+2)(x+3)
$$

$$
=x^2+5x+6
$$

Factoring:

$$
x^2+5x+6
$$

$$
=(x+2)(x+3)
$$

Always remember:

> **Multiplying creates an expanded polynomial. Factoring creates a product of factors.**

---

# 19. A Complete Polynomial Example

Simplify:

$$
2(x+3)(x+1)
$$

### Step 1: Multiply the binomials

$$
(x+3)(x+1)
$$

$$
=x^2+x+3x+3
$$

$$
=x^2+4x+3
$$

### Step 2: Multiply by 2

$$
2(x^2+4x+3)
$$

$$
\boxed{2x^2+8x+6}
$$

---

# 20. Common Mistakes

## Mistake 1: Combining Unlike Terms

Incorrect:

$$
3x^2+4x=7x^2
$$

These are not like terms.

The correct expression is:

$$
\boxed{3x^2+4x}
$$

---

## Mistake 2: Forgetting to Change Every Sign

Incorrect:

$$
(5x^2+2x)-(3x^2+4x)
$$

The correct expansion is:

$$
5x^2+2x-3x^2-4x
$$

not:

$$
5x^2+2x-3x^2+4x
$$

---

## Mistake 3: Multiplying Only the First Terms

Incorrect:

$$
(x+2)(x+3)=x^2+6
$$

Every term must be multiplied by every term.

Correct:

$$
\boxed{x^2+5x+6}
$$

---

## Mistake 4: Adding Exponents When Dividing

When multiplying:

$$
x^2\cdot x^3=x^5
$$

When dividing:

$$
\frac{x^5}{x^2}=x^3
$$

For division, subtract the exponents.

---

# 21. A Reliable Strategy

When working with polynomials:

### Adding

Remove parentheses and combine like terms.

### Subtracting

Distribute the negative sign first, then combine like terms.

### Multiplying

Multiply every term by every term.

### Dividing

Divide each term or divide coefficients and subtract exponents.

### Factoring

Find the greatest common factor and work backward from multiplication.

---

# GED Test Tip

The GED may ask you to:

* Add or subtract polynomial expressions.
* Expand products of binomials.
* Divide a polynomial by a monomial.
* Identify an equivalent polynomial expression.
* Factor a polynomial.
* Recognize the relationship between an expanded expression and its factored form.

Always check your work by using the opposite operation.

For example, if you factor:

$$
6x+12=6(x+2)
$$

check by distributing:

$$
6(x+2)=6x+12
$$

---

# Summary

When working with polynomials:

* **Add** by combining like terms.
* **Subtract** by distributing the negative sign and combining like terms.
* **Multiply** by distributing each term to every term.
* **Divide** coefficients and subtract exponents of like bases.
* **Factor** by finding common factors and reversing multiplication.

The most important rules are:

$$
\boxed{\text{Like terms can be combined.}}
$$

$$
\boxed{x^a\cdot x^b=x^{a+b}}
$$

$$
\boxed{\frac{x^a}{x^b}=x^{a-b}}
$$

$$
\boxed{a^2-b^2=(a-b)(a+b)}
$$
