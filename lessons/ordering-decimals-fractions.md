# GED Math Lesson: Ordering Fractions and Decimals

## Learning Goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

* Compare fractions and decimals.
* Convert fractions to decimals.
* Order numbers from least to greatest.
* Order numbers from greatest to least.
* Compare decimals using place value.
* Place numbers correctly on a number line.

---

# 1. Comparing Numbers

When comparing numbers, use these symbols:

$$
< \quad \text{less than}
$$

$$
> \quad \text{greater than}
$$

$$
= \quad \text{equal to}
$$

### Example

$$
3 < 7
$$

This means:

> 3 is less than 7.

And:

$$
9 > 4
$$

This means:

> 9 is greater than 4.

---

# 2. Converting Fractions to Decimals

A fraction can be converted to a decimal by dividing:

$$
\text{Numerator} \div \text{Denominator}
$$

Remember:

$$
\frac{\text{numerator}}{\text{denominator}}
$$

The numerator is divided by the denominator.

---

## Example

Convert:

$$
\frac{1}{2}
$$

to a decimal.

$$
1 \div 2 = 0.5
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\frac{1}{2}=0.5}
$$

---

## Another Example

Convert:

$$
\frac{3}{4}
$$

to a decimal.

$$
3 \div 4 = 0.75
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\frac{3}{4}=0.75}
$$

---

## Comparing the Fractions

Now compare:

$$
0.5 \quad \text{and} \quad 0.75
$$

Since:

$$
0.5 < 0.75
$$

we know:

$$
\boxed{\frac{1}{2}<\frac{3}{4}}
$$

---

# 3. Comparing Decimals Using Place Value

To compare decimals, compare the digits from left to right.

Compare:

$$
0.6,\quad 0.25,\quad 0.8
$$

Rewrite the numbers with the same number of decimal places:

$$
0.60,\quad 0.25,\quad 0.80
$$

Now compare the tenths and hundredths:

$$
0.25 < 0.60 < 0.80
$$

Therefore, from least to greatest:

$$
\boxed{0.25,\quad 0.6,\quad 0.8}
$$

---

# 4. Adding Zeros to Decimals

Adding zeros to the **end** of a decimal does not change its value.

For example:

$$
0.6 = 0.60 = 0.600
$$

However:

$$
0.06 \neq 0.6
$$

In fact:

$$
0.06 < 0.6
$$

### Why?

$$
0.6 = 0.60
$$

but:

$$
0.06
$$

has 6 hundredths, while:

$$
0.60
$$

has 60 hundredths.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{0.06 < 0.6}
$$

---

# 5. Ordering Fractions and Decimals Together

When a problem contains both fractions and decimals, convert them to the same form.

### Example

Order from least to greatest:

$$
\frac{1}{2},\quad 0.25,\quad \frac{3}{4}
$$

Convert the fractions to decimals:

$$
\frac{1}{2}=0.5
$$

$$
\frac{3}{4}=0.75
$$

Now compare:

$$
0.5,\quad 0.25,\quad 0.75
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{0.25,\quad \frac{1}{2},\quad \frac{3}{4}}
$$

---

# 6. Comparing Fractions Without Converting to Decimals

Sometimes you can compare fractions using a common denominator.

### Example

Compare:

$$
\frac{2}{3} \quad \text{and} \quad \frac{3}{4}
$$

The least common denominator is 12.

Rewrite both fractions:

$$
\frac{2}{3}=\frac{8}{12}
$$

$$
\frac{3}{4}=\frac{9}{12}
$$

Now compare:

$$
\frac{8}{12}<\frac{9}{12}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\frac{2}{3}<\frac{3}{4}}
$$

---

# 7. Placing Numbers on a Number Line

A number line shows numbers in order from left to right.

Numbers farther to the **left** are smaller.

Numbers farther to the **right** are larger.

For example:

$$
0 < 0.25 < 0.5 < 0.75 < 1
$$

---

## Example

Between which pair should $0.45$ be placed?

**A)** Between 0.3 and 0.4

**B)** Between 0.4 and 0.5

**C)** Between 0.5 and 0.6

Check the correct relationship:

$$
0.4 < 0.45 < 0.5
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{B) Between 0.4 and 0.5}}
$$

---

# 8. Ordering Numbers from Greatest to Least

Sometimes a problem asks for the reverse order.

### Example

Order from greatest to least:

$$
0.4,\quad \frac{3}{4},\quad 0.6
$$

Convert:

$$
\frac{3}{4}=0.75
$$

Now compare:

$$
0.75 > 0.6 > 0.4
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\frac{3}{4},\quad 0.6,\quad 0.4}
$$

---

# GED Tips

* Convert fractions to decimals when that makes comparison easier.
* Compare decimals from left to right.
* You can add zeros to the end of decimals.
* A number farther right on a number line is greater.
* Always check whether the question asks for:

  * **Least to greatest**
  * **Greatest to least**
* When comparing fractions, you can use:

  * Decimal conversion.
  * Common denominators.
  * Cross multiplication.

---

# Quick Review

To order fractions and decimals:

### Step 1

Convert all numbers to the same form if necessary.

### Step 2

Compare from left to right.

### Step 3

Arrange the numbers in the requested order.

Remember:

$$
0.5 = 0.50
$$

but:

$$
0.05 < 0.5
$$

---

# Memory Trick

> **Make them speak the same language.**

If you have fractions and decimals together, convert them all to decimals or all to fractions before comparing.
