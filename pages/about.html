GED INFORMATION

Everything You Need to Know About the GED

Learn about the GED test, its subjects, requirements, time limits, scores, and how to prepare.

What Is the GED?

The GED is a high school equivalency test. It measures whether you have knowledge and skills similar to those of a high school graduate.

The test consists of four separate subjects. You do not have to take all four subjects at the same time. You can prepare for and take each subject separately.

GED Requirements

GED requirements can vary depending on where you take the test. In general, test-takers may need to meet requirements related to:

Age Requirements

You must meet the minimum age requirement established by your testing jurisdiction.

School Enrollment

Some jurisdictions have rules about whether you can still be enrolled in high school.

Identification

You may need an approved form of identification when registering for or taking the test.

Local Rules

Requirements, testing options, and policies may differ depending on your location.

The Four GED Subjects

The GED test has four subjects. Each subject tests different skills that are important for college, careers, and everyday life.

Mathematical Reasoning

This subject tests your ability to solve mathematical problems and apply mathematics to real-world situations.

Topics include:
  • Basic math
  • Fractions, decimals, and percentages
  • Algebra
  • Geometry
  • Graphs and data
  • Probability and statistics

Reasoning Through Language Arts

This subject tests your ability to understand written information, analyze arguments, and communicate clearly.

Skills include:
  • Reading comprehension
  • Analyzing arguments
  • Grammar and language
  • Editing sentences
  • Writing an extended response

Science

This subject tests your ability to understand scientific information and use evidence to solve problems.

Topics include:
  • Life science
  • Physical science
  • Earth and space science
  • Scientific experiments
  • Graphs and data interpretation

Social Studies

This subject tests your ability to understand history, government, economics, and information presented in graphs and documents.

Topics include:
  • United States history
  • Government and civics
  • Economics
  • Geography
  • Reading historical documents

How Much Time Does the GED Take?

The GED consists of four separate tests. Each subject has its own time limit.

Mathematical Reasoning

115 minutes

Reasoning Through Language Arts

150 minutes

Science

90 minutes

Social Studies

70 minutes

Why Does Each Subject Matter?

Mathematics

Helps you solve problems, analyze information, manage money, and make logical decisions.

Language Arts

Helps you understand information, communicate ideas, and evaluate arguments.

Science

Helps you understand the natural world and use evidence and reasoning to solve problems.

Social Studies

Helps you understand society, government, history, economics, and the world around you.

How the GED Works

1

Choose a Subject

Begin with the subject you are most prepared to study.

2

Study and Practice

Learn the concepts and practice answering GED-style questions.

3

Take the Test

Take the subject test when you feel prepared.

4

Continue Your Journey

Pass the remaining subjects and work toward your goals.

Tips for Preparing for the GED

  • Study consistently instead of waiting until the last minute.
  • Practice with questions similar to the real test.
  • Review your mistakes and understand why your answer was incorrect.
  • Focus more time on your weakest subjects and topics.
  • Practice managing your time while answering questions.
